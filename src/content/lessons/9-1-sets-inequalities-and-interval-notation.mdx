<Callout type="why">
An equation like $2x + 1 = 7$ has one answer. An inequality like $2x + 1 < 7$ has infinitely many — every number less than $3$. Real limits are like that: a budget of *at most* 50 dollars, a speed of *no more than* 120 km/h, a grade of *at least* 85. We need compact, precise ways to write such **sets** of numbers, and reliable rules for finding them.
</Callout>

## Three ways to write a set

| Inequality | Set-builder notation | Interval notation |
|---|---|---|
| $-2 \le x < 4$ | $\{x \mid -2 \le x < 4\}$ | $[-2, 4)$ |
| $x > 1$ | $\{x \mid x > 1\}$ | $(1, \infty)$ |
| $x \le 3$ | $\{x \mid x \le 3\}$ | $(-\infty, 3]$ |

Set-builder notation is read "the set of all $x$ such that …". In interval notation, a bracket $[\ ]$ means the endpoint is **included**; a parenthesis $(\ )$ means it is **not**. $\infty$ is not a number you can reach, so it always takes a parenthesis. On a number line, a filled dot is an included endpoint and an open dot is an excluded one.

## Solving inequalities — and why the sign can flip

The **addition principle** works exactly as for equations: adding or subtracting the same number on both sides keeps the inequality true, because it slides both numbers the same distance along the number line.

The **multiplication principle** has one twist. Multiplying both sides by a **positive** number keeps the order. Multiplying by a **negative** number reverses it:

$$2 < 5, \qquad \text{but} \qquad -1 \cdot 2 = -2 \;>\; -5 = -1 \cdot 5.$$

Multiplying by $-1$ reflects every number across $0$, so whatever was further right ends up further left. That is why:

> Multiplying or dividing both sides by a **negative** number **reverses** the inequality sign.

## In a real context

Inequality words have exact meanings:

| Words | Symbol | Example |
|---|---|---|
| at least, no less than, a minimum of | $\ge$ | at least 18 years old: $a \ge 18$ |
| at most, no more than, a maximum of | $\le$ | at most 50 dollars: $c \le 50$ |
| more than, exceeds | $>$ | more than 3 hours: $t > 3$ |
| less than, under | $<$ | under 12: $a < 12$ |

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Forgetting to reverse the sign">
From $-3x \le 12$, dividing by $-3$ gives $x \ge -4$, not $x \le -4$. Check with a number: $x = 0$ should make the original true.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Reversing when you shouldn't">
Only **multiplying or dividing by a negative** reverses the sign. Subtracting $5$ — even from a negative number — doesn't. Neither does dividing a negative number by a positive one: from $3x < -12$, $x < -4$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="A bracket on infinity">
$[-2, \infty]$ is wrong: $\infty$ is not a number that can be included. Write $[-2, \infty)$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="“At least” as “more than”">
"At least 85" includes $85$: use $\ge$, and a bracket in interval notation.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Inequality, set-builder and interval notation describe the same set; $[\ ]$ includes an endpoint, $(\ )$ excludes it, $\pm\infty$ always gets $(\ )$.
- Add or subtract anything; multiply or divide by a positive number freely.
- Multiplying or dividing by a **negative** reverses the sign, because it reflects the number line.
- "At least" is $\ge$, "at most" is $\le$; check one number from your answer in the original inequality.
