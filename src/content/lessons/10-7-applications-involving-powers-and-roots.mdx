## Right triangles — and why a² + b² = c²

In a right triangle with legs $a$, $b$ and hypotenuse $c$ (the side opposite the right angle, always the longest):

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

**Why it's true.** Put four copies of the triangle inside a square of side $a + b$, so their hypotenuses form a tilted square of side $c$ in the middle. Count the big square's area two ways:

$$(a + b)^2 = c^2 + 4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}ab \quad\Rightarrow\quad a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = c^2 + 2ab \quad\Rightarrow\quad a^2 + b^2 = c^2.$$

Give **exact** answers as simplified radicals; type them like `2sqrt(21)` or `sqrt(84)` (both are accepted here).

## Distance between two points

Two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ are the ends of the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose legs are horizontal and vertical, with lengths $|x_2 - x_1|$ and $|y_2 - y_1|$. The Pythagorean theorem gives

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}.$$

Squaring removes the signs, so the order of the points doesn't matter.

## Special right triangles

Two triangles appear so often that it pays to know their side ratios — and both come straight from the Pythagorean theorem.

- **45°–45°–90°** (half a square): the legs are equal, $a$ and $a$, so $c^2 = a^2 + a^2 = 2a^2$ and the hypotenuse is $a\sqrt{2}$.
- **30°–60°–90°** (half an equilateral triangle of side $2a$): the hypotenuse is $2a$, the shorter leg (opposite $30°$) is $a$, and the longer leg is $\sqrt{(2a)^2 - a^2} = \sqrt{3a^2} = a\sqrt{3}$.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Use the ratios"
  problem="(a) A 45°–45°–90° triangle has hypotenuse $10$. Find a leg. (b) A 30°–60°–90° triangle has shorter leg $4$. Find the other sides."
  steps={[
    { show: '(a) $a\\sqrt{2} = 10 \\;\\Rightarrow\\; a = \\dfrac{10}{\\sqrt{2}} = \\dfrac{10\\sqrt{2}}{2} = 5\\sqrt{2}$', why: 'Rationalize the denominator (10.5).' },
    { show: '(b) longer leg $4\\sqrt{3}$, hypotenuse $2 \\cdot 4 = 8$', why: 'Check: $4^2 + (4\\sqrt{3})^2 = 16 + 48 = 64 = 8^2$ ✓.' },
  ]}
/>

<QuickCheck

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Using the hypotenuse as a leg">
The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and is always the longest side. In the ladder problem, $4^2 + 10^2 = h^2$ is wrong: the ladder ($10$) is the hypotenuse.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Adding the sides instead of their squares">
$c \neq a + b$. With legs $3$ and $4$, the hypotenuse is $5$, not $7$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Mixing up the 30°–60°–90° legs">
The **shorter** leg is opposite $30°$; the **longer** leg is $\sqrt{3}$ times it; the hypotenuse is **twice** it.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ (area argument: $(a + b)^2 = c^2 + 2ab$); the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle.
- Distance formula = Pythagorean theorem on the grid: $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$.
- 45°–45°–90°: $a, a, a\sqrt{2}$. 30°–60°–90°: $a, a\sqrt{3}, 2a$.
- Give exact answers in simplified radical form, and estimate to check.
