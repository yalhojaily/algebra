<Callout type="why">
A ball's height, a shop's revenue and a bakery's profit all follow quadratic functions, and they almost always come written as $ax^2 + bx + c$, not in vertex form. The questions people ask about them — *How high does it go? When? What price earns the most?* — are all questions about one point: the **vertex**. This lesson shows how to find it straight from $a$, $b$ and $c$, and why the formula works.
</Callout>

## Where is the vertex — and why?

In 11.5 you read the vertex straight off $f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$. For $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ it is hidden, but **symmetry** finds it.

A parabola is symmetric about a vertical line through its vertex, the **axis of symmetry**. So if two points of the graph have the **same height**, the axis is exactly **halfway** between them. Two such points are easy to find:

- At $x = 0$: $\;f(0) = c$.
- At $x = -\dfrac{b}{a}$: $\;f\!\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right) = a \cdot \dfrac{b^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{b^2}{a} + c = \dfrac{b^2}{a} - \dfrac{b^2}{a} + c = c$.

Both points have height $c$. Halfway between $0$ and $-\frac{b}{a}$ is

$$x = -\frac{b}{2a}.$$

That is the axis of symmetry, and the vertex sits on it. Its height is $f\!\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right)$:

$$\text{vertex} = \left(-\frac{b}{2a},\ f\!\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right)\right)$$

### Maximum or minimum?

The sign of $a$ tells you which way the parabola opens — no calculation needed:

- $a > 0$: opens **up**, so the vertex is the lowest point: $f$ has a **minimum** value.
- $a < 0$: opens **down**, so the vertex is the highest point: $f$ has a **maximum** value.

The maximum or minimum **value** is the $y$-coordinate of the vertex. Move the sliders and watch the vertex, the axis and the intercepts follow $a$, $b$ and $c$.

## Vertex form by completing the square

Sometimes you want the whole function in vertex form $a(x - h)^2 + k$, for example to see every transformation at once. Completing the square gets there. When $a \neq 1$, **factor $a$ out of the $x$-terms first**, so the inside starts with plain $x^2$.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Complete the square inside a function"
  problem="Write $\;f(x) = 2x^2 - 12x + 13\;$ in vertex form."
  steps={[
    { show: '$= 2(x^2 - 6x) + 13$', why: 'Factor $a = 2$ out of the $x$-terms only. The $13$ stays outside.' },
    { show: '$= 2(x^2 - 6x + 9 - 9) + 13$', why: 'Add and subtract $\\left(\\frac{-6}{2}\\right)^2 = 9$ inside. Adding $0$ changes nothing.' },
    { show: '$= 2(x - 3)^2 - 18 + 13$', why: 'Group $x^2 - 6x + 9 = (x - 3)^2$. The $-9$ is still inside the parentheses, so it is multiplied by $2$ when it comes out: $-18$.' },
    { show: '$= 2(x - 3)^2 - 5$', why: 'Check with the formula: $-\\frac{b}{2a} = \\frac{12}{4} = 3$ and $f(3) = 18 - 36 + 13 = -5$. ✓' },
  ]}
/>

## Intercepts

- **$y$-intercept:** set $x = 0$. It is always $(0, c)$.
- **$x$-intercepts:** solve $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, by factoring or the quadratic formula.

The discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ (from 11.4) tells you in advance how many $x$-intercepts there are: positive gives **two**, zero gives **one** (the vertex sits on the $x$-axis), negative gives **none**.

  ]}

## Putting it together: sketching the graph

To sketch $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ by hand:

1. **Direction:** the sign of $a$ (up or down).
2. **Vertex** $\left(-\frac{b}{2a}, f\!\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right)\right)$ and the axis of symmetry.
3. **$y$-intercept** $(0, c)$ — and its **mirror point** across the axis, for free.
4. **$x$-intercepts**, if there are any.
5. Draw a smooth U through the points.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Losing the minus sign in −b/(2a)">
For $f(x) = x^2 + 6x + 1$, the vertex is at $x = -\frac{6}{2} = -3$, not $3$. Write the formula out with every sign before you simplify.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Stopping at the x-coordinate">
The vertex is a **point**. "The maximum value of $f$" is its $y$-coordinate $f\!\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right)$, not $-\frac{b}{2a}$. In context: if the question asks *how high*, $t = 2$ is not the answer — $69$ ft is.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Forgetting to multiply by a when completing the square">
$2(x^2 - 6x + 9 - 9) + 13 \neq 2(x - 3)^2 - 9 + 13$. The $-9$ was inside the parentheses, so it becomes $2 \cdot (-9) = -18$. Correct: $2(x - 3)^2 - 5$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Mixing up maximum and minimum">
$a > 0$ (opens up) gives a **minimum**; $a < 0$ (opens down) gives a **maximum**. Decide this first, from the sign of $a$ alone.
</Callout>

## Summary

- The axis of symmetry is $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$: halfway between $x = 0$ and $x = -\frac{b}{a}$, which have the same height $c$.
- Vertex: $\left(-\frac{b}{2a}, f\!\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right)\right)$. $a > 0$ gives a minimum value, $a < 0$ a maximum value.
- Vertex form by completing the square: factor out $a$ first, and multiply what you subtract by $a$.
- $y$-intercept $(0, c)$; $x$-intercepts from $f(x) = 0$; the discriminant says how many.
- "When / what price / how many" asks for the first coordinate of the vertex; "how high / how much" asks for the second.
