<Callout type="why">
Quadratic functions usually show up as $ax^2 + bx + c$, not in vertex form. You don't need to convert every time: the vertex's $x$-coordinate is always $-\frac{b}{2a}$.
</Callout>

## The vertex

For $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ the vertex is

$$\left(-\frac{b}{2a},\ f\!\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right)\right)$$

## Intercepts

- **$y$-intercept:** $(0, c)$.
- **$x$-intercepts:** solve $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$. There may be two, one or none.

<Practice client:visible lesson="11.6" skill="intercepts" compact />

## Summary

- Vertex: $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$, then $y = f(x)$.
- Vertex form via completing the square (factor out $a$ first).
- $y$-intercept $(0, c)$; $x$-intercepts from $f(x) = 0$.
