## The principle of powers

> If $a = b$, then $a^n = b^n$.

But the converse fails: $(-3)^2 = 3^2$ even though $-3 \ne 3$. So raising both sides to an even power can introduce solutions that don't satisfy the original equation — **extraneous** solutions. Every answer must be checked.

## One radical

1. Isolate the radical on one side.
2. Square (or cube) both sides.
3. Solve.
4. **Check** in the original equation.

<Callout type="warning" title="Isolate first">
Squaring $\sqrt{x - 3} + 5$ as it stands gives $(x - 3) + 10\sqrt{x - 3} + 25$ — the radical doesn't go away. Get the radical alone **before** squaring.
</Callout>

## Two radicals

If each side has one radical, squaring removes both: $\sqrt{3x - 4} = \sqrt{2x + 3}$ gives $3x - 4 = 2x + 3$, so $x = 7$. Check that the radicands are not negative.

## Summary

- Isolate the radical, then raise both sides to the index.
- Squaring can create extraneous solutions — check every answer in the original equation.
- Cube roots: cube both sides; no extraneous solutions arise.
