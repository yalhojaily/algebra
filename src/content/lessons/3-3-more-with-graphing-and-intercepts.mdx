<Callout type="why">
A water tank holds $600$ liters and drains at $40$ liters per minute: $40t + w = 600$. When is it empty? When is it full? Those are the two **intercepts** — and for many real problems they are exactly the questions that matter.
</Callout>

## x- and y-intercepts

<Callout type="definition" title="Intercepts">
- The **$y$-intercept** is where a graph crosses the $y$-axis. There, $x = 0$. To find it, **set $x = 0$** and solve for $y$.
- The **$x$-intercept** is where a graph crosses the $x$-axis. There, $y = 0$. To find it, **set $y = 0$** and solve for $x$.
</Callout>

For $Ax + By = C$ this is quick: setting $x = 0$ just "covers up" the $x$-term.

<Callout type="warning" title="Which zero goes where?">
The $x$-intercept has the form $(a, 0)$ and the $y$-intercept has the form $(0, b)$. Mixing them up is the most common mistake in this lesson.
</Callout>

## Horizontal and vertical lines

Set $A = 0$ or $B = 0$ in the explorer above and watch what happens.

<Callout type="definition" title="Horizontal and vertical lines">
- The graph of $y = b$ is a **horizontal** line through $(0, b)$. Every point on it has $y$-coordinate $b$.
- The graph of $x = a$ is a **vertical** line through $(a, 0)$. Every point on it has $x$-coordinate $a$.
</Callout>

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Why y = −3 is horizontal"

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Through the origin">
If both intercepts are $(0, 0)$, you need one more point to draw the line.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="y = b or x = a?">
$y = -3$ is horizontal; $x = -3$ is vertical. The letter that appears tells you which coordinate is fixed.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Intercepts as numbers">
Write intercepts as points: the $x$-intercept of $2x + 5y = 10$ is $(5, 0)$, not just $5$ — unless the question asks for the number.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $y$-intercept: set $x = 0$. $x$-intercept: set $y = 0$. Plot both and connect them.
- If a line passes through the origin, both intercepts are $(0, 0)$ — find one more point.
- $y = b$ is horizontal; $x = a$ is vertical.
