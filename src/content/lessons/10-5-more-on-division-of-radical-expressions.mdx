<Callout type="why">
$\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ are the same number. The second form is the standard one: it's easier to estimate ($1.414 \div 2$), easier to compare, and it's what answers in the back of the book look like.
</Callout>

## The quotient rule

$$\frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} = \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} \qquad\text{e.g.}\quad \frac{\sqrt{75}}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{25} = 5$$

## Rationalizing one-term denominators

To **rationalize** a denominator means to rewrite the expression so the denominator has no radicals. Multiply the top and bottom by the radical in the denominator — this is multiplying by $1$.

## Two-term denominators: use the conjugate

Multiplying $\sqrt{3} + 1$ by $\sqrt{3}$ doesn't remove the radical. Multiplying by its **conjugate** $\sqrt{3} - 1$ does:

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Conjugate"
  problem="Rationalize: $\;\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{5} - 1}$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$= \\dfrac{6}{\\sqrt{5} - 1}\\cdot\\dfrac{\\sqrt{5} + 1}{\\sqrt{5} + 1}$', why: 'Multiply by the conjugate over itself.' },
    { show: '$= \\dfrac{6(\\sqrt{5} + 1)}{5 - 1} = \\dfrac{6(\\sqrt{5} + 1)}{4}$', why: '$(\\sqrt{5} - 1)(\\sqrt{5} + 1) = 5 - 1$.' },
    { show: '$= \\dfrac{3\\sqrt{5} + 3}{2}$', why: 'Reduce by $2$.' },
  ]}
/>

## Summary

- $\sqrt{a}/\sqrt{b} = \sqrt{a/b}$.
- One-term denominator $\sqrt{b}$: multiply by $\frac{\sqrt{b}}{\sqrt{b}}$.
- Two-term denominator: multiply by the conjugate over itself.
