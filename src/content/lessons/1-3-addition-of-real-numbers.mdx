## Addition on the number line

To find $a + b$ on a number line:

1. **Start** at $a$.
2. If $b$ is **positive**, move $b$ units to the **right**. If $b$ is **negative**, move $|b|$ units to the **left**. If $b$ is $0$, stay put.
3. The number you **land on** is the sum.

Try it. Watch which way the arrow goes when the second number is negative.

## Addition without a number line

Drawing a line every time is slow. Look at the patterns from the number line and you'll find just two rules.

<Callout type="tip" title="The two rules of addition">
**Same signs:** add the absolute values. The answer has that same sign.

$$-5 + (-8) = -13 \qquad 5 + 8 = 13$$

**Different signs:** subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger. The answer takes the sign of the number with the **larger** absolute value.

$$-11 + 4 = -7 \qquad 11 + (-4) = 7$$

If the numbers are opposites, the sum is $0$.
</Callout>

### Why the rules work: zero pairs

Think of $+1$ and $-1$ as counters that cancel each other, because $1 + (-1) = 0$. When you add numbers with different signs, the pairs cancel and **only the extra counters are left** — that's why you subtract and keep the sign of the "bigger" group.

## Opposites (additive inverses)

Two numbers whose sum is $0$ are **opposites**, or **additive inverses**: $7$ and $-7$, $-2.5$ and $2.5$, $0$ and $0$.

<Callout type="definition" title="The opposite of a number">
The opposite of $x$ is written $-x$ (read "the opposite of $x$"). For every real number $x$:

$$x + (-x) = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad -(-x) = x$$
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="−x is not always negative">
$-x$ means *the opposite of $x$*, not *a negative number*. If $x = -6$, then $-x = -(-6) = 6$, which is positive. Read $-x$ as "the opposite of $x$", never as "negative $x$".
</Callout>

## Adding several numbers

Addition can be done in any order and with any grouping (you'll see why in Lesson 1.7). So when you have many numbers, a quick method is:

1. Add all the **positive** numbers.
2. Add all the **negative** numbers.
3. Add those two results.

## Watch out for these

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">
1. **Adding when the signs differ.** $-9 + 4$ is $-5$, not $-13$. Different signs → subtract.
2. **Taking the wrong sign.** In $6 + (-10)$, the negative number is "bigger", so the answer is negative: $-4$.
3. **Reading −x as "a negative number".** If $x$ is negative, $-x$ is positive.
</Callout>

## Summary

- On the number line, adding a positive moves **right**; adding a negative moves **left**.
- **Same signs:** add absolute values, keep the sign. **Different signs:** subtract absolute values, take the sign of the larger.
- **Opposites** add to $0$: $x + (-x) = 0$, and $-(-x) = x$.
- With many numbers, add the positives, add the negatives, then combine.
