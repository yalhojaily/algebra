## The strategy

1. **Common factor?** Always factor out the GCF first (and $-1$ if the leading coefficient is negative).
2. **Count the terms.**
   - **Two terms:** difference of squares? sum or difference of cubes? (A sum of squares is prime.)
   - **Three terms:** trinomial square? If not, use product–sum ($a = 1$) or FOIL / the ac-method ($a \ne 1$).
   - **Four terms:** factor by grouping.
3. **Factor completely.** Check every factor again — can it be factored further?
4. **Check** by multiplying.

<Callout type="warning" title="Stopping too early">
The most common error in this lesson is leaving a factor that still factors — like $(x^2 - 9)$ or a GCF hiding inside parentheses, as in $(2x + 4)(x - 1)$, which is $2(x + 2)(x - 1)$.
</Callout>
