## The strategy

1. **Common factor?** Always factor out the GCF first (and $-1$ if the leading coefficient is negative).
2. **Count the terms.**
   - **Two terms:** difference of squares? sum or difference of cubes? (A sum of squares is prime.)
   - **Three terms:** trinomial square? If not, use product–sum ($a = 1$) or FOIL / the ac-method ($a \ne 1$).
   - **Four terms:** factor by grouping.
3. **Factor completely.** Check every factor again — can it be factored further?
4. **Check** by multiplying.

</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Skipping the GCF">
$3x^2 - 27$ is not a difference of squares as it stands — $3(x^2 - 9) = 3(x + 3)(x - 3)$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Factoring a sum of squares">
$x^2 + 4$ is prime. Only differences of squares factor.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Not checking">
Multiply your answer out. If you don't get the original polynomial back, something went wrong.
</Callout>

## Summary

- GCF → count terms → apply the method → check each factor again → check by multiplying.
