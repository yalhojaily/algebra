<Callout type="why">
Calculators have only two log keys: LOG (base 10) and LN (base $e$). Why $e$, a strange number like $2.71828\ldots$? Because it is what you get when growth happens **continuously** — every instant instead of once a year — and that is how populations, radioactive decay and continuously compounded interest really behave. And with the change-of-base formula, those two keys are enough to compute any logarithm.
</Callout>

## Where e comes from

Invest $1$ dollar at $100\%$ interest for a year. Compounded once, you get $2$. Compounded $n$ times, you get $\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$ — more, because interest earns interest sooner. But it doesn't grow without limit: as $n$ grows, the amount levels off at a special number,

$$e = \lim_{n \to \infty}\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n \approx 2.718281828.$$

<EApproach client:visible />

So compounding **continuously** at rate $r$ multiplies the money by $e^r$ each year: $A = Pe^{rt}$.

## Natural logarithms

The logarithm with base $e$ is the **natural logarithm**, $\ln x = \log_e x$. All the rules from 12.4 hold, and:

$$\ln e^x = x \qquad e^{\ln x} = x \qquad \ln 1 = 0 \qquad \ln e = 1$$

Type `ln(x)` and `e^(2x)`.

## Change of base

How do you find $\log_3 20$ with only LOG and LN keys? Let $x = \log_3 20$. Then $3^x = 20$. Take $\ln$ of both sides and use the power rule: $x\ln 3 = \ln 20$, so

$$\log_b M = \frac{\ln M}{\ln b} = \frac{\log M}{\log b}$$

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="ln e = 0">
$\ln e = 1$, because $e^1 = e$. It is $\ln 1$ that equals $0$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Change of base upside down">
$\log_b M = \frac{\ln M}{\ln b}$: the number you take the log **of** goes on top.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="ln of a sum">
$\ln(x + y) \ne \ln x + \ln y$ — the same as for any logarithm.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $e = \lim\limits_{n \to \infty}\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n \approx 2.71828$: the result of continuous compounding; $A = Pe^{rt}$.
- $\ln x = \log_e x$; $\ln e^x = x$, $e^{\ln x} = x$, $\ln 1 = 0$, $\ln e = 1$.
- $\log_b M = \frac{\ln M}{\ln b}$, from taking $\ln$ of $b^x = M$.
