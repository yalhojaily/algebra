<Callout type="why">
A taxi charges a 10-riyal starting fee plus 2 riyals per kilometer. What does a 7 km ride cost? A 12 km ride? A 30 km ride?

You could work each one out separately. Or you could write **one rule** that covers every possible ride:

$$
\text{cost} = 2\htmlClass{c-var}{k} + 10
$$

where $\htmlClass{c-var}{k}$ is the number of kilometers. That single line *is* algebra: using a letter to stand for a number that can change, so you can describe a whole pattern at once.
</Callout>

## Variables and constants

A **variable** is a letter that represents a number that can *vary*. In the taxi rule, $\htmlClass{c-var}{k}$ is a variable — it is 7 on one ride and 30 on another.

A **constant** is a number that stays the same. In the taxi rule, $\htmlClass{c-num}{2}$ and $\htmlClass{c-num}{10}$ are constants: the price per kilometer and the starting fee do not change from ride to ride.

<Callout type="definition" title="Variable vs. constant">
- **Variable** — a letter that can stand for different numbers: $x$, $n$, $t$, $k$, …
- **Constant** — a fixed value: $2$, $10$, $-3$, $\pi$, …

Any letter can be a variable. Choosing a letter that reminds you of its meaning ($t$ for time, $c$ for cost) makes your work easier to read.
</Callout>

## Algebraic expressions

An **algebraic expression** combines constants and variables using operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. Here are some examples:

$$
t + 37 \qquad 2k + 10 \qquad \frac{a}{b} \qquad 5x - 9y \qquad lw
$$

### How multiplication and division are written

Algebra rarely uses the $\times$ sign, because it looks too much like the letter $x$. Instead, all of these mean "**3 times x**":

<table class="nice">
  <thead><tr><th>Written as</th><th>Meaning</th><th>Notes</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>$3x$</td><td>$3$ times $x$</td><td>The most common form: a number written right before a letter.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>$3 \cdot x$</td><td>$3$ times $x$</td><td>A raised dot means multiply.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>$3(x)$ or $(3)(x)$</td><td>$3$ times $x$</td><td>Parentheses next to each other mean multiply.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>$ab$</td><td>$a$ times $b$</td><td>Two letters side by side are multiplied.</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

Division is usually written with a fraction bar. These all mean "**8 divided by t**":

$$
8 \div t \qquad 8/t \qquad \frac{8}{t}
$$

<Callout type="warning" title="3x is not thirty-something">
When $x = 4$, the expression $3x$ means $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ — **not** $34$. Writing a number next to a letter means *multiply*; it never means "put the digits side by side."
</Callout>

### Expression or equation?

An **expression** is like a phrase: it names a quantity, such as $2k + 10$. An **equation** is like a sentence: it uses an equals sign to say that two expressions have the same value, such as $2k + 10 = 24$. You *evaluate* or *simplify* an expression; you *solve* an equation.

## Evaluating expressions

To **evaluate** an expression, you **substitute** a number for each variable and then calculate. The result is called the *value* of the expression.

## Translating phrases into expressions

Word problems are written in English, but you solve them in algebra. Translating is a skill of its own, and it comes down to recognizing **key words**.

<table class="nice">
  <thead><tr><th>Addition $+$</th><th>Subtraction $-$</th><th>Multiplication $\cdot$</th><th>Division $\div$</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>added to</td><td>subtracted from ⇄</td><td>multiplied by</td><td>divided by</td></tr>
    <tr><td>sum</td><td>difference</td><td>product</td><td>quotient</td></tr>
    <tr><td>plus</td><td>minus</td><td>times</td><td>per</td></tr>
    <tr><td>more than</td><td>less than ⇄</td><td>twice ($2\cdot$)</td><td>ratio</td></tr>
    <tr><td>increased by</td><td>decreased by</td><td>of (with % or fractions)</td><td>split into</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

The phrases marked ⇄ **reverse the order**: the number mentioned *first* is written *second*. Pick a phrase below and watch the colors: each word turns into the symbol of the same color.

<TranslateLab client:visible />

<Callout type="warning" title="“Less than” flips the order">
"$5$ less than $x$" means *start with $x$ and take $5$ away*: $\;x - 5$.

It is **not** $5 - x$. Test it with a real number: "$5$ less than $20$" is $15$, and $20 - 5 = 15$, while $5 - 20 = -15$. ✔
</Callout>

## Equations and solutions

An **equation** is a number sentence that says two expressions are equal. It contains an equals sign, $=$. Some equations are true, some are false, and some are neither until you know the value of the variable:

$$
3 + 4 = 7 \;\;(\text{true}) \qquad 9 - 2 = 5 \;\;(\text{false}) \qquad x + 5 = 12 \;\;(\text{depends on } x)
$$

<Callout type="definition" title="Solution">
A **solution** of an equation is a value of the variable that makes the equation **true**. To check whether a number is a solution, **substitute** it and see whether both sides come out equal.
</Callout>

Think of an equation as a **balance scale**. The left side goes on one pan and the right side on the other. A number is a solution exactly when the scale balances. Slide $x$ until it does:

## Translating to equations

Many word problems become an equation once you notice that **"is"**, **"equals"**, and **"is the same as"** all translate to $=$, and **"what number"** (or "a number") becomes a variable.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="From a sentence to an equation"
  problem="Translate: **What number plus 478 is 1019?**"
  steps={[
    { show: '"What number" $\\;\\rightarrow\\; x$', why: 'Choose a letter for the unknown.' },
    { show: '"plus 478" $\\;\\rightarrow\\; + \\,478$', why: '"Plus" means add.' },
    { show: '"is 1019" $\\;\\rightarrow\\; = 1019$', why: '"Is" becomes the equals sign.' },
  ]}
  answer="$x + 478 = 1019$. (You'll learn to solve equations like this in Chapter 2. For now, notice that $541$ works: $541 + 478 = 1019$.)"
/>

    { show: '$12n = 84$', why: 'The total cost was 84 riyals.' },

## Watch out for these

<Callout type="warning" title="Five mistakes to avoid">
1. **Gluing digits together.** If $x = 4$, then $3x = 12$, not $34$.
2. **Flipping "less than" the wrong way.** "$7$ less than $y$" is $y - 7$.
3. **Forgetting parentheses.** "Twice the sum of $x$ and $3$" is $2(x + 3)$, not $2x + 3$.
4. **Swapping a division.** "$x$ divided by $4$" is $\dfrac{x}{4}$, not $\dfrac{4}{x}$.
5. **Calling an expression an equation.** $2x + 1$ has no equals sign, so there is nothing to "solve."
</Callout>

## Summary

- A **variable** is a letter that stands for a changing number; a **constant** is a fixed number.
- An **algebraic expression** combines numbers and variables with operations. $3x$, $3 \cdot x$, and $3(x)$ all mean multiplication; $\frac{a}{b}$ means division.
- To **evaluate** an expression, substitute values for the variables and calculate.
- Translate phrases using key words — and remember that "less than" and "subtracted from" **flip the order**.
- An **equation** says two expressions are equal. A **solution** is a value that makes it true; check it by substituting.
