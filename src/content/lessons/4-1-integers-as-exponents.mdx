<Callout type="why">
Bacteria in a dish double every hour: after $h$ hours there are $2^h$ times as many. After $3$ more hours *on top of* $5$ hours, there are $2^5 \cdot 2^3$ times as many. And $2$ hours *before* we started? That needs $2^{-2}$. The rules in this lesson make calculations like these instant.
</Callout>

## Exponential notation, and exponents 0 and 1

In $a^n$, the **base** $a$ is used as a factor $n$ times: $a^4 = a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a$. Two special cases follow from the patterns below:

<Callout type="definition" title="Exponents of 1 and 0">
$$a^1 = a \qquad\qquad a^0 = 1 \;\;\text{(for } a \ne 0\text{)}$$
</Callout>

## The product and quotient rules

Pick a rule and change the exponents. Every rule comes straight from writing out the factors.

<ExponentLab client:visible />

<Callout type="tip" title="Product rule and quotient rule">
For any number $a$ (with $a \ne 0$ when dividing) and integers $m$, $n$:

$$a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n} \qquad\qquad \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$$

Multiplying powers of the **same base**: **add** exponents. Dividing: **subtract** them.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Same base only">
$x^3 \cdot y^4$ can't be combined — the bases are different. And $2^3 \cdot 2^4 = 2^7$, **not** $4^7$: the base stays the same.
</Callout>

## Negative exponents

Look at the "Negative exponents" tab above: going down the pattern divides by the base each time, so a negative exponent means a **reciprocal**.

<Callout type="definition" title="Negative exponents">
For any non-zero $a$ and integer $n$:

$$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \qquad\qquad \frac{1}{a^{-n}} = a^n$$
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="A negative exponent is not a negative number">
$2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}$, not $-8$. And in $5x^{-2} = \frac{5}{x^2}$, only the $x$ moves — the exponent belongs to $x$, not to $5$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $a^1 = a$, $\;a^0 = 1$ ($a \ne 0$), $\;a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$.
- **Product rule:** $a^m a^n = a^{m+n}$. **Quotient rule:** $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$.
- Final answers are usually written with **positive** exponents.
