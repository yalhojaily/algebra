<Callout type="why">
$2^x = 8$ is easy: $x = 3$. But what about $2^x = 10$? We need a name for "the exponent on 2 that gives 10." That name is $\log_2 10$. Logarithms also tame numbers that span huge ranges: the acidity of lemon juice and of soap differ by a factor of a billion, but on the pH scale they are just 2 and 11.
</Callout>

## The definition

$$\log_b a = c \quad\text{means}\quad b^c = a \qquad (b > 0,\ b \ne 1,\ a > 0)$$

**A logarithm is an exponent.** $\log_b a$ answers the question "**$b$ to what power is $a$?**" The logarithm with base $10$ is written simply $\log a$ (the **common logarithm**).

## Evaluating logarithms

To find $\log_b a$, write $a$ as a power of $b$.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Evaluate"
  problem="Find $\;\log 1000,\;\; \log_2 \tfrac{1}{8},\;\; \log_9 3,\;\; \log_7 1,\;\; \log_7 7$."
  steps={[
    { show: '$\\log 1000 = 3$', why: '$10^3 = 1000$.' },
    { show: '$\\log_2 \\tfrac{1}{8} = -3$', why: '$\\frac{1}{8} = 2^{-3}$. Logs of numbers between $0$ and $1$ are negative.' },
    { show: '$\\log_9 3 = \\tfrac{1}{2}$', why: '$9^{1/2} = \\sqrt{9} = 3$.' },
    { show: '$\\log_7 1 = 0$ and $\\log_7 7 = 1$', why: '$7^0 = 1$ and $7^1 = 7$ — true for every base.' },
  ]}
/>

Type logarithms as `log_2(8)`, `log(1000)` (base 10) or `ln(x)`.

## The logarithmic function

Since $\log_b a$ is the exponent that undoes "raise $b$ to a power", $f(x) = \log_b x$ is the **inverse** of $g(x) = b^x$ (12.2): its graph is the graph of $b^x$ reflected in $y = x$. The inputs and outputs swap, so:

- Domain of $\log_b x$: $(0, \infty)$ — the range of $b^x$. You can only take the logarithm of a **positive** number.
- Range: all real numbers. The $y$-axis is a vertical asymptote, and the graph passes through $(1, 0)$.

## In a real context

Logarithmic scales turn "times 10" into "plus 1", so huge ranges become manageable numbers.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Mixing up the parts">
$\log_b a = c$ means $b^c = a$: the **base** stays the base. $\log_2 8 = 3$ is $2^3 = 8$, not $3^2 = 8$ or $8^3 = 2$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Logs of zero or negative numbers">
$\log 0$ and $\log(-5)$ are undefined: no power of a positive base is $0$ or negative.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Dividing instead">
$\log_2 8$ is not $\frac{8}{2}$. Ask "2 to what power is 8?"
</Callout>

## Summary

- $\log_b a = c \iff b^c = a$: a logarithm is an exponent.
- $\log a$ means $\log_{10} a$; $\log_b 1 = 0$, $\log_b b = 1$.
- $\log_b x$ is the inverse of $b^x$: domain $(0, \infty)$, range all reals, graph through $(1, 0)$.
- Logarithmic scales (pH, decibels, magnitude) turn multiplication by $10$ into adding $1$.
