## Graphs of linear equations

Equations whose graphs are straight lines are called **linear equations**. Any equation that can be written as $y = mx + b$ or $Ax + By = C$ is linear.

To graph one:
1. **Choose** several $x$-values (three is a good habit: two determine the line, the third checks your work).
2. **Compute** $y$ for each and write a table.
3. **Plot** the points and **draw** the line through them, with arrows on both ends — the line goes on forever.

## The y-intercept

The point where a line crosses the $y$-axis is its **$y$-intercept**. Every point on the $y$-axis has $x = 0$ — so substitute $x = 0$:

$$y = m \cdot 0 + b = b$$

<Callout type="definition" title="y-intercept of y = mx + b">
The graph of $y = mx + b$ crosses the $y$-axis at $(0, b)$.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Plotting only two points">
Two points always make a line — even if one is wrong. A third point checks your arithmetic.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Signs when substituting">
For $y = -x + 4$ at $x = -1$: $y = -(-1) + 4 = 5$. Use parentheses.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Solving for y halfway">
From $5y = -3x + 10$, divide **every** term by $5$: $y = -\frac{3}{5}x + 2$.
</Callout>


## Summary

- Linear equations ($y = mx + b$, $Ax + By = C$) graph as straight lines.
- Make a table with at least three points; the third is a check.
- The $y$-intercept of $y = mx + b$ is $(0, b)$.
