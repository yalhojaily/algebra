<Callout type="why">
Expressions like $\dfrac{1 + \frac{1}{x}}{1 - \frac{1}{x^2}}$ show up when you combine rates or average speeds. They look messy, but one move — multiplying by the LCD — cleans them up.
</Callout>

## Method 1: multiply by the LCD

A **complex rational expression** has rational expressions in its numerator, its denominator, or both.

1. Find the LCD of **all** the little denominators.
2. Multiply the numerator and the denominator of the big fraction by that LCD (this is multiplying by $1$).
3. Distribute — every little fraction clears.
4. Factor and simplify.

## Method 2: divide

You can also write the top and the bottom as **single** fractions, then divide (multiply by the reciprocal):

$$\frac{1 + \frac{1}{x}}{1 - \frac{1}{x^2}} = \frac{\frac{x + 1}{x}}{\frac{x^2 - 1}{x^2}} = \frac{x + 1}{x} \cdot \frac{x^2}{(x + 1)(x - 1)} = \frac{x}{x - 1}$$

Both methods give the same answer — use whichever feels easier.

## Summary

- LCD method: multiply top and bottom by the LCD of all the little fractions.
- Division method: make the top and bottom single fractions, then multiply by the reciprocal.
- Finish by factoring and cancelling.
