<Callout type="why">
Trial and error can take many guesses when $a$ and $c$ have lots of factors. The **ac-method** (also called *grouping*) turns the problem into the product–sum search you already know from 5.2, followed by the grouping you learned in 5.1.
</Callout>

## The four steps

To factor $ax^2 + bx + c$ (after taking out any GCF):

1. Multiply $a \cdot c$.
2. Find two numbers $m$ and $n$ with $m \cdot n = ac$ and $m + n = b$.
3. Split the middle term: $bx = mx + nx$.
4. Factor by grouping.

<Callout type="tip" title="Order doesn't matter">
Splitting $11x$ as $9x + 2x$ instead works too: $6x^2 + 9x + 2x + 3 = 3x(2x + 3) + 1(2x + 3) = (2x + 3)(3x + 1)$. Same factors.

## Summary

- $ac$ → product–sum pair → split $bx$ → group.
- If no pair exists, the trinomial is prime.
- Take out the GCF before you start, and check by multiplying.
