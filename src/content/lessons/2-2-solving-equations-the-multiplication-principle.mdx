<Callout type="why">
Six friends split a restaurant bill equally and each pays $45$ riyals. What was the bill? If $b$ is the bill, then $\dfrac{b}{6} = 45$. Or: $4$ identical notebooks cost $38$ riyals — so $4n = 38$. In both equations the variable is being **multiplied or divided**, and adding or subtracting can't undo that.
</Callout>

## The multiplication principle

<Callout type="definition" title="The multiplication principle for equations">
For any real numbers $a$, $b$, and any **non-zero** number $c$:

$$a = b \quad\text{is equivalent to}\quad a \cdot c = b \cdot c$$

You may multiply (or divide) **both sides** by the same non-zero number.
</Callout>

Why not zero? Multiplying both sides by $0$ turns *any* equation into $0 = 0$, which throws away all the information.

To undo multiplication by $a$, **divide** by $a$ (or multiply by its reciprocal $\frac{1}{a}$). To undo division by $a$, **multiply** by $a$.

## Fraction and decimal coefficients

When the coefficient is a fraction, the fastest move is to multiply both sides by its **reciprocal**, because a number times its reciprocal is $1$.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Multiply by the reciprocal"
  problem="Solve: $\;\dfrac{3}{4}y = -12$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$\\dfrac{4}{3} \\cdot \\dfrac{3}{4}y = \\dfrac{4}{3} \\cdot (-12)$', why: 'The reciprocal of $\\frac{3}{4}$ is $\\frac{4}{3}$.' },

## Which principle?

Look at what is being done **to** the variable, then undo it:

<table class="nice">
  <thead><tr><th>Equation</th><th>Done to x</th><th>Undo it by…</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>$x + 9 = 4$</td><td>add $9$</td><td>subtracting $9$ (addition principle)</td></tr>
    <tr><td>$x - 2 = 10$</td><td>subtract $2$</td><td>adding $2$ (addition principle)</td></tr>
    <tr><td>$9x = 4$</td><td>multiply by $9$</td><td>dividing by $9$ (multiplication principle)</td></tr>
    <tr><td>$\frac{x}{2} = 10$</td><td>divide by $2$</td><td>multiplying by $2$ (multiplication principle)</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

## Watch out for these

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">
1. **Subtracting the coefficient.** In $5x = 30$, the $5$ multiplies $x$. Divide by $5$ — don't subtract it.
2. **Losing a sign.** $-3x = 12$ gives $x = -4$, not $4$.
3. **Leaving $-x$.** $-x = 6$ means $x = -6$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- **Multiplication principle:** $a = b$ is equivalent to $ac = bc$ for any ${c \neq 0}$.
- Undo multiplication by dividing; undo division by multiplying.
- With a fraction coefficient, multiply both sides by its **reciprocal**.
