Quick: what is $4 \cdot 37 \cdot 25$? If you multiply left to right, it's tedious. But regroup it as $(4 \cdot 25) \cdot 37 = 100 \cdot 37 = 3700$ and it's instant. The properties in this lesson are the **legal moves** of algebra — they tell you exactly how you may rearrange an expression without changing its value.

## Equivalent expressions

Two expressions are **equivalent** if they have the same value for **every** allowed replacement of the variables. For example, $3x + 2x$ and $5x$ are equivalent: try $x = 1, 2, -7, \ldots$ — they always match.

<Callout type="definition" title="The identity properties">
- **Identity property of 0:** $a + 0 = a$. Adding $0$ changes nothing.
- **Identity property of 1:** $a \cdot 1 = a$. Multiplying by $1$ changes nothing.
</Callout>

The identity property of $1$ is how we write equivalent fractions: multiply by a clever form of $1$, such as $\tfrac{5}{5}$.

$$
\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{5}{5} = \frac{15}{20}
$$

## Commutative and associative laws

<Callout type="definition" title="Commutative laws — order doesn't matter">
$$a + b = b + a \qquad\qquad ab = ba$$
</Callout>

<Callout type="definition" title="Associative laws — grouping doesn't matter">
$$a + (b + c) = (a + b) + c \qquad\qquad a(bc) = (ab)c$$
</Callout>

A memory aid: you **commute** when you travel — things change **places**. **Associates** are the people you group with — things change **groups**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Not for subtraction or division">
$7 - 3 \ne 3 - 7$ and $8 \div 2 \ne 2 \div 8$. The commutative and associative laws work only for **addition** and **multiplication**. (That's one reason to rewrite subtraction as adding the opposite.)
</Callout>

## The distributive law

The distributive law connects multiplication and addition. It's the most used property in all of algebra.

<Callout type="definition" title="The distributive law">
$$a(b + c) = ab + ac \qquad\qquad a(b - c) = ab - ac$$

Multiply the number outside the parentheses by **each** term inside.
</Callout>

Why is it true? Think of area. A rectangle that is $a$ wide and $(b + c)$ long can be split into two rectangles. Move the sliders — then switch one length to $x$.

## Factoring

**Factoring** is the distributive law run **backwards**: you start with a sum and write it as a product.

$$
\underbrace{6(2x + 3)}_{\text{factored}} \;\xrightarrow{\text{distribute}}\; \underbrace{12x + 18}_{\text{expanded}} \;\xrightarrow{\text{factor}}\; 6(2x + 3)
$$

To factor completely, take out the **greatest common factor** (GCF) — the largest number that divides every term.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Factoring out the GCF"
  problem="Factor: $\;16a - 24b + 8$"
  steps={[
    { show: 'GCF of $16$, $24$, $8$ is $8$', why: '$8$ divides all three, and nothing larger does.' },
    { show: '$= 8 \\cdot 2a - 8 \\cdot 3b + 8 \\cdot 1$', why: 'Write each term as $8$ times something.' },
    { show: '$= 8(2a - 3b + 1)$', why: 'Use the distributive law backwards. Don\'t forget the $1$!' },
  ]}
  answer="$8(2a - 3b + 1)$. Check by distributing: $16a - 24b + 8$ ✓"
/>

<Callout type="tip" title="Always check by multiplying back">
Distribute your answer. If you get the original expression, the factoring is right. Also check that nothing more can come out of the parentheses: $4(4a - 6b + 2)$ is equivalent, but not **completely** factored.
</Callout>

## Collecting like terms

**Terms** are the parts of an expression separated by $+$ signs (after rewriting subtraction as adding the opposite). **Like terms** have exactly the same variable part: $3x$ and $-7x$ are like terms; $3x$ and $3y$ are not; $x^2$ and $x$ are not.

To **collect** (or *combine*) like terms, add their coefficients. This is the distributive law again: $3x + 5x = (3 + 5)x = 8x$.

## Watch out for these

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">
1. **Distributing to only the first term.** $5(x - 2) = 5x - 10$.
2. **Losing a negative when distributing.** $-2(x - 3) = -2x + 6$, because $(-2)(-3) = 6$.
3. **Not factoring completely.** $18x + 12 = 6(3x + 2)$, not $2(9x + 6)$.
4. **Combining unlike terms.** $3x + 2y$ cannot be simplified, and $x^2 + x \ne x^3$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- **Identity:** $a + 0 = a$, $\;a \cdot 1 = a$.
- **Commutative:** $a + b = b + a$, $\;ab = ba$. **Associative:** $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$, $\;(ab)c = a(bc)$.
- **Distributive:** $a(b + c) = ab + ac$. Used forwards to **multiply**, and backwards to **factor**.
- **Like terms** have the same variable part; combine them by adding coefficients.
