## The formula

The solutions of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ (with $a \ne 0$) are

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

## Standard form first

The formula needs the equation in the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$. Move everything to one side first: $x^2 + 2x = 5 - 2x$ becomes $x^2 + 4x - 5 = 0$.

## Complex solutions

When $b^2 - 4ac < 0$, the square root is imaginary and the solutions are complex conjugates $p \pm qi$.

## Summary

- $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$ solves every quadratic equation in standard form.
- Simplify the radical and reduce the fraction.
- A negative discriminant gives complex conjugate solutions.
