## The exponential model

$$P(t) = P_0 e^{kt}$$

$P_0$ is the amount at $t = 0$, and $k$ is the **relative growth rate**: when $k = 0.03$, the quantity grows by about $3\%$ of its current size per unit of time. That is the defining feature of exponential change — the bigger it is, the faster it grows (or, with $k < 0$, the faster it shrinks). If $k > 0$ it's **exponential growth**; if $k < 0$, **exponential decay**.

## Doubling time and half-life

Setting $P(T) = 2P_0$: $P_0e^{kT} = 2P_0$, so $e^{kT} = 2$ and

$$T = \frac{\ln 2}{k} \qquad\text{(doubling time; for decay, the half-life is } \tfrac{\ln 2}{|k|})$$

$P_0$ cancels: the doubling time is the same no matter how much you start with. That's why "doubles every 25 years" is a meaningful description.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Doubling time from the rate"
  problem="An investment grows at $7\%$ compounded continuously. How long does it take to double?"
  steps={[
    { show: '$T = \\dfrac{\\ln 2}{0.07} \\approx \\dfrac{0.6931}{0.07} \\approx 9.90$ years', why: 'Bankers\' "rule of 70": doubling time $\\approx 70 \\div$ (rate in %).' },
  ]}
/>

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="The rate from the doubling time"
  problem="A town's population doubles every $25$ years. Find the growth rate $k$."

## Building a model from data

Two data points are enough to find $P_0$ and $k$.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Fit and predict"
  problem="A city had $1000$ (thousand) people in 2000 and $1500$ (thousand) in 2010. Assuming exponential growth, predict its population in 2030."
  steps={[
    { show: '$P(t) = 1000e^{kt}$ with $t$ = years after 2000', why: '$P_0 = 1000$ from the 2000 data.' },
    { show: '$1500 = 1000e^{10k} \\;\\Rightarrow\\; k = \\dfrac{\\ln 1.5}{10} \\approx 0.0405$', why: 'Use the 2010 data to find $k$.' },
    { show: '$P(30) = 1000e^{30k} = 1000\\left(e^{10k}\\right)^3 = 1000(1.5)^3 = 3375$', why: 'Every 10 years the population is multiplied by $1.5$.' },
    { show: 'About $3.4$ million people in 2030.', why: 'A model is only as good as its assumption: growth may slow.' },
  ]}
/>

## Half-life and radioactive dating

A substance with half-life $H$ satisfies $A(t) = A_0\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/H}$ — the same model, written with base $\frac{1}{2}$: every $H$ years, half of what is left decays.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Carbon dating"
  problem="Carbon-14 has a half-life of $5730$ years. A bone has $70\%$ of the carbon-14 of living bone. How old is it?"
  steps={[
    { show: '**Familiarize.** $A(t) = A_0\\left(\\tfrac{1}{2}\\right)^{t/5730}$, and $A = 0.7A_0$.', why: 'Less than half has decayed, so it is younger than one half-life (5730 years).' },
    { show: '**Translate.** $\\left(\\tfrac{1}{2}\\right)^{t/5730} = 0.7$', why: '$A_0$ cancels.' },
    { show: '**Solve.** $\\dfrac{t}{5730}\\ln 0.5 = \\ln 0.7 \\;\\Rightarrow\\; t = 5730 \\cdot \\dfrac{\\ln 0.7}{\\ln 0.5} \\approx 5730(0.5146) \\approx 2948$', why: 'Take ln, power rule, divide.' },
    { show: '**Check.** $0.5^{2948/5730} = 0.5^{0.5146} \\approx 0.70$ ✓, and $2948 < 5730$ as expected.', why: '' },
    { show: '**State.** The bone is about $2900$ years old.', why: '' },
  ]}
/>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="The rate as a whole number">
A $3\%$ growth rate is $k = 0.03$, not $3$. With $k = 3$, the town would grow twentyfold in a single year.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="The sign of k">
Decay needs $k < 0$. With a half-life, $k = -\frac{\ln 2}{H}$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="The half-life exponent">
In $A_0\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/H}$ the exponent is $\frac{t}{H}$ — the number of half-lives — not $t$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $P(t) = P_0e^{kt}$: $k$ is the relative growth rate; growth for $k > 0$, decay for $k < 0$.
- Doubling time / half-life $= \frac{\ln 2}{|k|}$, independent of the starting amount.
- Two data points determine $P_0$ and $k$; logarithms solve for $t$ or $k$.
- $A_0\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/H}$ models decay with half-life $H$.
