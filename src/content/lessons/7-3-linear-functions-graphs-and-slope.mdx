<Callout type="why">
A phone plan costs 20 dollars a month plus a 15-dollar sign-up fee. After $t$ months you've paid $C(t) = 20t + 15$. That's a **linear function**: the $20$ is how fast the cost grows (the slope), and the $15$ is where it starts (the $y$-intercept).
</Callout>

## Slope–intercept form

A **linear function** has the form

$$f(x) = mx + b$$

- $m$ is the **slope**: the change in $f(x)$ when $x$ increases by $1$.
- $(0, b)$ is the **$y$-intercept**: the output when the input is $0$.

## Linear models

In applications, the slope is a **rate** (dollars per month, centimeters per hour), and the $y$-intercept is the **starting value**.

## Summary

- $f(x) = mx + b$: slope $m$, $y$-intercept $(0, b)$.
- To graph: plot $(0, b)$, then use rise over run.
- In a model, slope = rate of change; $b$ = starting value.
