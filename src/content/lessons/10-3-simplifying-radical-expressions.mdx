## The product rule

$$\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\cdot\sqrt{b} \qquad \sqrt[3]{ab} = \sqrt[3]{a}\cdot\sqrt[3]{b}$$

To simplify, write the radicand as a **perfect square** (or cube) times what's left:

## Variables under the radical

Assuming the variables are nonnegative, $\sqrt{x^2} = x$, $\sqrt{x^4} = x^2$, and in general an even power comes out with half its exponent. An odd power leaves one factor inside: $\sqrt{x^5} = \sqrt{x^4}\sqrt{x} = x^2\sqrt{x}$.

Type `5x sqrt(2x)` — the parentheses show exactly what is under the radical. `sqrt2x` would mean $\sqrt{2}\cdot x$.
</Callout>

## Multiplying radicals

$\sqrt{a}\cdot\sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$ — then simplify: $\sqrt{6}\cdot\sqrt{10} = \sqrt{60} = 2\sqrt{15}$.

## Summary

- $\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$: take out perfect-square (perfect-cube) factors.
- Simplified form: no perfect-power factors left under the radical.
- Multiply radicals by multiplying radicands, then simplify.
