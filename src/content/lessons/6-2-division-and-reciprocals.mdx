$\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{1} = \frac{3}{2}$ — "keep, change, flip." Dividing rational expressions works exactly the same way.
</Callout>

## Reciprocals

Two expressions are **reciprocals** if their product is $1$. The reciprocal of $\frac{A}{B}$ is $\frac{B}{A}$, and the reciprocal of a polynomial $P$ is $\frac{1}{P}$.

$$\frac{x + 2}{x - 5} \cdot \frac{x - 5}{x + 2} = 1 \qquad (x^2 - 3) \cdot \frac{1}{x^2 - 3} = 1$$

## Dividing

$$\frac{A}{B} \div \frac{C}{D} = \frac{A}{B} \cdot \frac{D}{C}$$

Flip **only the second** fraction (the divisor), then multiply, factor and cancel.

<RationalLab client:visible mode="divide" />

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Divide"
  problem="Divide: $\;\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + x} \div \dfrac{x - 3}{x + 1}$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$= \\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + x} \\cdot \\dfrac{x + 1}{x - 3}$', why: 'Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.' },
    { show: '$= \\dfrac{(x - 3)(x + 3)(x + 1)}{x(x + 1)(x - 3)}$', why: 'Factor everything.' },
    { show: '$= \\dfrac{x + 3}{x}$', why: 'Cancel $(x - 3)$ and $(x + 1)$.' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Flip the right fraction">
Only the fraction **after** the $\div$ sign is flipped. Flipping the first one (or both) gives a different answer.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Cancelling across the ÷ sign">
In $\dfrac{x}{4} \div \dfrac{x}{2}$ you can't cancel the top $x$ with the top $x$. Flip the divisor first; then cancel.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Excluded values from the divisor">
In $\dfrac{A}{B} \div \dfrac{C}{D}$, $C$ can't be $0$ either — after flipping, it is in the denominator.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Not simplifying">
After multiplying by the reciprocal, factor and cancel — the job isn't done until nothing more cancels.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Reciprocal of $\frac{A}{B}$ is $\frac{B}{A}$.
- $\frac{A}{B} \div \frac{C}{D} = \frac{A}{B} \cdot \frac{D}{C}$ — then factor and cancel.
