Problems with two unknowns are often easier with two variables than with one: each fact in the problem becomes its own simple equation.
</Callout>

## The five steps, with two variables

1. **Familiarize** — name the two unknowns.
2. **Translate** — write **two** equations (often: one counts *how many*, the other counts *how much*).
3. **Solve** the system.
4. **Check** in the original words.
5. **State** the answer.

## Mixtures

A mixture problem has the same structure: one equation for the **amount** of liquid, one for the **amount of the ingredient** (acid, salt, …).

## Motion

With a current (or wind), speeds add one way and subtract the other: downstream $b + c$, upstream $b - c$. Use $d = rt$ for each trip.

## Summary

- Two unknowns → two variables → two equations.
- Tables help: one row per item, columns for amount, rate, and total.
- Check the answer against the words of the problem.
