## LCM of numbers

The **least common multiple (LCM)** is the smallest number that each of the numbers divides into.

1. Write each number as a product of primes.
2. Use each prime the **greatest** number of times it occurs in any **one** factorization.

## LCM of expressions

The same rule works for algebraic factors. For monomials, take the LCM of the numbers and the **highest power** of each variable: the LCM of $6x^2y$ and $4xy^3$ is $12x^2y^3$.

## Rewriting with the LCD

To give a fraction the LCD as its denominator, multiply its top and bottom by the **missing factors** — this is multiplying by $1$:

$$\frac{5}{x + 3} = \frac{5}{x + 3} \cdot \frac{x - 1}{x - 1} = \frac{5(x - 1)}{(x + 3)(x - 1)}$$

## Summary

- Factor each denominator completely.
- The LCD uses each factor the greatest number of times it appears in any one denominator.
- Rename a fraction by multiplying top and bottom by the factors its denominator is missing.
