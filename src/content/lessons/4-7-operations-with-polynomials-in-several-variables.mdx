<Callout type="why">
Most real formulas have more than one variable. The surface area of a can depends on its radius **and** its height: $S = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$. The volume of a box depends on three lengths. Nothing new is needed — every rule from 4.3–4.6 still works. The only new thing to watch is what makes terms "like".

## Degree

The **degree of a term** is the **sum** of the exponents of its variables: $5x^2y^3$ has degree $2 + 3 = 5$. The degree of the polynomial is still the largest degree of its terms.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Degrees"
  problem="Find the degree of each term and of $\;3x^4y - 7x^2y^2 + xy + 2$."
  steps={[
    { show: 'Terms: degree $5$, $4$, $2$, $0$', why: '$x^4y$: $4 + 1$. $x^2y^2$: $2 + 2$. $xy$: $1 + 1$. A constant: $0$.' },
    { show: 'The polynomial has degree $5$.', why: '' },
  ]}
/>

  client:visible
  question="What is the degree of $\;5x^2y^3 - 2xy$?"

## Like terms, adding and subtracting

**Like terms** have exactly the same variables with exactly the same exponents. $3xy^2$ and $-xy^2$ are like terms; $3xy^2$ and $3x^2y$ are **not**.

<QuickCheck
  client:visible
  question="Which pair are like terms?"
  options={['$3xy^2$ and $-xy^2$', '$3xy^2$ and $3x^2y$', '$2ab$ and $2a$', '$x^2y$ and $x^2y^2$']}
  answer={0}
  explain="Both are $xy^2$ — same variables, same exponents. Only the coefficients differ."
  wrong={{ 1: 'In one term $y$ is squared; in the other $x$ is squared.', 2: 'One term has $b$ and the other doesn\'t.', 3: 'The exponents of $y$ differ: $1$ and $2$.' }}
/>

## Multiplying

The rule is the same: every term times every term. With several variables, use the product rule **variable by variable**. The box method keeps the products organized:

<StepReveal

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="xy² and x²y are not like terms">
Check **every** exponent. $3xy^2 + 2x^2y$ cannot be combined.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Degree of a term">
The degree of $5x^2y^3$ is $2 + 3 = 5$ — add the exponents of all its variables, don't take the largest one.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Order of variables">
$xy$ and $yx$ **are** like terms (multiplication is commutative): $3xy + 2yx = 5xy$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Degree of a term = sum of its exponents; degree of a polynomial = largest term degree.
- Like terms have the same variables with the same exponents.
- Add, subtract and multiply exactly as before; FOIL and the special products still apply.
