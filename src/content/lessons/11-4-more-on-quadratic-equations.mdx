## The discriminant

| $b^2 - 4ac$ | Solutions | Graph of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ |
|---|---|---|
| positive | two real | crosses the $x$-axis twice |
| zero | one real (repeated) | touches the $x$-axis at the vertex |
| negative | two complex conjugates | doesn't reach the $x$-axis |

## Writing equations from solutions

If the solutions are $r$ and $s$, then $(x - r)(x - s) = 0$ is an equation with those solutions. Multiply it out.

## Equations quadratic in form

An equation is **quadratic in form** if a substitution $u = (\text{something})$ makes it $au^2 + bu + c = 0$.

With $u = \sqrt{x}$, an equation like $x - 5\sqrt{x} + 6 = 0$ becomes $u^2 - 5u + 6 = 0$. (Check answers — $u$ must be $\ge 0$.)

## Summary

- $b^2 - 4ac > 0$: two real; $= 0$: one; $< 0$: two complex.
- Solutions $r, s$ → $(x - r)(x - s) = 0$.
- Substitute to reveal a hidden quadratic; then substitute back.
