<Callout type="why">
The path of a basketball, the spray of a fountain, the cable of a suspension bridge and the cross-section of a satellite dish are all parabolas. In **vertex form** you can read the most important point — the top or the bottom — straight from the equation, and sketch the whole graph in seconds.
</Callout>

## The effect of a, h and k

$$f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$$

Start from $y = x^2$, whose vertex is $(0, 0)$:

- **$k$ shifts up** $k$ units (down if $k < 0$): every output gets $k$ added.
- **$h$ shifts right** $h$ units (left if $h < 0$). Why right, when the formula *subtracts* $h$? In $y = x^2$ the lowest point is at input $0$. In $(x - h)^2$ the inside is $0$ when $x = h$, so the lowest point has moved to $x = h$. Everything happens $h$ units **later**.
- **$a$ stretches**: $|a| > 1$ makes the graph narrower, $|a| < 1$ wider; $a < 0$ flips it to open **down**.

So the vertex is $(h, k)$ and the axis of symmetry is the line $x = h$.

## Axis of symmetry and extreme values

The parabola is symmetric about the vertical line $x = h$. If $a > 0$, the vertex is the lowest point, so $k$ is the **minimum** value of $f$; if $a < 0$, $k$ is the **maximum** value.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="The sign of h">
$f(x) = (x + 3)^2$ is $(x - (-3))^2$: it moves **left** 3, and the vertex is $(-3, 0)$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Narrow or wide backwards">
$|a| > 1$ is **narrower**, $|a| < 1$ is **wider**. Test: $y = 3x^2$ at $x = 1$ is already $3$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Maximum or minimum">
The sign of $a$ decides: $a < 0$ opens down (maximum $k$); $a > 0$ opens up (minimum $k$). The maximum **value** is $k$, not $h$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$: vertex $(h, k)$, axis $x = h$. $h$ shifts right (the turning point comes $h$ units later), $k$ shifts up.
- $a > 0$: opens up, minimum $k$; $a < 0$: opens down, maximum $k$; $|a|$ controls the width.
- Graph with the vertex and a few points on one side; mirror them across the axis.
