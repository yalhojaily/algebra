<Callout type="why">
Factoring (5.8) solves only the quadratic equations that happen to factor nicely. $x^2 - 6x + 4 = 0$ doesn't. This lesson builds a method that works for **every** quadratic: turn it into "something squared equals a number", then take square roots. It leads straight to the quadratic formula in 11.2.
</Callout>

## The principle of square roots

> If $x^2 = k$, then $x = \sqrt{k}$ or $x = -\sqrt{k}$ — written $x = \pm\sqrt{k}$.

Why both? Both $\sqrt{k}$ and $-\sqrt{k}$ square to $k$. Factoring confirms there are no others: $x^2 - k = (x - \sqrt{k})(x + \sqrt{k}) = 0$. If $k = 0$ there is one solution, $0$. If $k < 0$ the solutions are imaginary (10.8): $x^2 = -9$ gives $x = \pm 3i$.

Type $\pm$ as `+-`, for example `+- 2sqrt(3)` or `3 +- sqrt(5)`.

## Completing the square

The idea: turn $x^2 + bx$ into a perfect square $(x + \square)^2$ by adding the right number. Picture $x^2 + bx$ as an $x$-by-$x$ square plus a $b$-by-$x$ rectangle. Cut the rectangle in half and put one half on each side of the square: you get an almost-square of side $x + \frac{b}{2}$, missing only a corner of size $\frac{b}{2} \times \frac{b}{2}$.

$$x^2 + bx + \left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 = \left(x + \tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2$$

## Solving by completing the square

1. Get $x^2 + bx$ alone on one side. If $a \ne 1$, **divide by $a$ first**.
2. Add $\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2$ to **both** sides.
3. Write the left side as a square, then use the principle of square roots.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Forgetting ±">
$x^2 = 49$ has two solutions, $\pm 7$. The symbol $\sqrt{49}$ is just $7$, but the **equation** has both.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Adding to one side only">
When completing the square, add $\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2$ to **both** sides, or the equation changes.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Not dividing by a first">
For $2x^2 + 8x - 1 = 0$, adding $\left(\frac{8}{2}\right)^2 = 16$ does not make a perfect square. Divide by $2$ first.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Half of b, or b/2 squared?">
The number to **add** is $\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2$; the number **inside** the parentheses is $\frac{b}{2}$: $x^2 + 10x + 25 = (x + 5)^2$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $x^2 = k \Rightarrow x = \pm\sqrt{k}$ (imaginary if $k < 0$); $(x - h)^2 = k \Rightarrow x = h \pm \sqrt{k}$.
- Completing the square adds the missing corner $\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2$ to **both** sides; divide by $a$ first.
- Every quadratic equation can be solved this way.
- In context, reject solutions that make no sense (negative lengths, negative growth factors).
