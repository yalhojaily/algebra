## What a fraction means

In $\dfrac{3}{8}$ the **denominator** $8$ says the whole is cut into 8 equal pieces; the **numerator** $3$ says how many pieces you have. The denominator names the *size* of the pieces, the numerator *counts* them.

## Same denominator: count the pieces

When the denominators match, the pieces are the same size, so you just add or subtract the counts:

$$\frac{a}{d} + \frac{b}{d} = \frac{a + b}{d} \qquad \frac{a}{d} - \frac{b}{d} = \frac{a - b}{d}$$

## Different denominators: make the pieces match

You can't add thirds to quarters directly — they're different sizes. Cut both into a common size first. The best choice is the **least common denominator (LCD)**: the least common multiple of the denominators (see [A.2](../a-2-least-common-multiple-lcm/)).

1. Find the LCD.
2. Rewrite each fraction with the LCD by multiplying top and bottom by the same number.
3. Add or subtract the numerators.
4. Simplify.

## Multiplying: no common denominator needed

$$\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot c}{b \cdot d}$$

"$\frac{1}{2}$ of $\frac{3}{4}$" means $\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{4}$. Switch the bars widget to **Multiply** to see why: the overlap of the two shaded strips is the product.

You can simplify **before** multiplying by canceling a factor from any numerator with any denominator — it keeps the numbers small.

## Whole numbers and fractions

A whole number is a fraction with denominator 1: $5 = \frac{5}{1}$.

- **Multiplying:** $5 \cdot \frac{2}{15} = \frac{10}{15} = \frac{2}{3}$.
- **Adding or subtracting:** rewrite the whole number with the fraction's denominator: $2 + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{8}{4} + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning">
**Adding the denominators.** $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} \neq \frac{2}{7}$. The denominator is the piece size — it doesn't get added. Correct: $\frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Changing only the denominator.** Rewriting $\frac{2}{3}$ as $\frac{2}{12}$ changes its value. Whatever you multiply the bottom by, multiply the top by the same number: $\frac{2}{3} = \frac{8}{12}$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Finding a common denominator to multiply.** Multiplication doesn't need one. $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}$ directly.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Leaving the answer unreduced.** $\frac{8}{12}$ is correct but not finished: divide by the GCD to get $\frac{2}{3}$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Same denominator: add or subtract the numerators; keep the denominator.
- Different denominators: rewrite with the LCD, then combine the numerators.
- Multiply: numerator × numerator over denominator × denominator; cancel common factors first if you like.
- Always reduce the answer to lowest terms.
