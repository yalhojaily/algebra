<Callout type="why">
A water tank loses $4$ liters every hour through a leak. How has the amount of water changed after $6$ hours? Repeated change is multiplication: $6 \cdot (-4)$. And "$3$ hours **ago**, the tank had how much more water?" leads to $(-3) \cdot (-4)$ — two negatives.
</Callout>

## A positive times a negative

Multiplication of whole numbers is repeated addition: $3 \cdot 5 = 5 + 5 + 5$. The same idea works with a negative:

$$
3 \cdot (-5) = (-5) + (-5) + (-5) = -15
$$

Three losses of $5$ is a loss of $15$. And because order doesn't matter in multiplication, $(-5) \cdot 3 = -15$ too.

<Callout type="tip" title="Different signs">
The product of a positive number and a negative number is **negative**. Multiply the absolute values and make the answer negative.
</Callout>

## A negative times a negative

Repeated addition can't explain $(-3) \cdot (-5)$ — you can't add something "negative three times". Instead, follow a **pattern**. Pick a negative first factor, then reveal the rows one by one. Before each click, predict the next product.

<SignPattern client:visible />

The pattern only continues if a negative times a negative is **positive**. Mathematicians define it this way so that all the other rules of arithmetic keep working.

<Callout type="tip" title="The sign rules for multiplication">
- **Same signs** → the product is **positive**: $(-6)(-7) = 42$
- **Different signs** → the product is **negative**: $(-6)(7) = -42$
- Any number times $0$ is $0$.
</Callout>

## Three or more factors

Multiply two numbers at a time — or use a shortcut: **every pair of negative factors makes a positive**. So you only need to **count** the negatives.

<Callout type="tip" title="Counting negatives">
If none of the factors is $0$:
- an **even** number of negative factors gives a **positive** product;
- an **odd** number of negative factors gives a **negative** product.
</Callout>

## Watch out for these

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">
1. **Using the addition rules.** $(-4)(-5) = 20$, not $-20$. With multiplication, two negatives make a **positive**.
2. **Miscounting negatives.** In $(-1)(-1)(-1)$ there are three, so the product is $-1$.
3. **Forgetting zero.** If **any** factor is $0$, the product is $0$, no matter how many negatives there are.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Same signs → positive product. Different signs → negative product.
- For many factors, count the negatives: even → positive, odd → negative (unless a factor is $0$).
- Repeated change = number of times × change each time.
