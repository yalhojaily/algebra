## Multiplying monomials

To multiply monomials, multiply the **coefficients** and use the **product rule** for each variable (add the exponents). The commutative and associative laws let us regroup:

$$(3x^4)(-2x^3) = (3 \cdot (-2))(x^4 \cdot x^3) = -6x^7$$

## A monomial times a polynomial

Use the **distributive law**: multiply the monomial by **each** term.

## Two binomials

Distribute twice: $(x + 3)(x + 2) = x(x + 2) + 3(x + 2) = x^2 + 2x + 3x + 6 = x^2 + 5x + 6$. Each term of the first binomial multiplies each term of the second — **four** products. An area model shows why: a rectangle with sides $x + 3$ and $x + 2$ splits into four smaller rectangles.

## Any two polynomials

<Callout type="tip" title="The rule">
To multiply two polynomials, multiply **every term of one by every term of the other**, then collect like terms. A polynomial with $m$ terms times one with $n$ terms gives $m \cdot n$ products before collecting.
</Callout>

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Binomial times trinomial"
  problem="Multiply: $\;(2x - 1)(x^2 + x - 3)$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$2x(x^2 + x - 3) - 1(x^2 + x - 3)$', why: 'Distribute each term of the binomial.' },
    { show: '$= 2x^3 + 2x^2 - 6x - x^2 - x + 3$', why: 'Six products.' },
    { show: '$= 2x^3 + x^2 - 7x + 3$', why: 'Collect like terms.' },
    { show: 'Check with $x = 1$: $\\;(1)(-1) = -1$ and $2 + 1 - 7 + 3 = -1$ ✓', why: 'Substituting a number is a quick check.' },
  ]}
/>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Distributing to only one term">
$-3x^2(2x^2 - 5x + 4)$ needs **three** products. Stopping after the first ($-6x^4$) is a common slip.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="(x + 3)(x + 2) is not x² + 6">
Multiplying only the first terms and the last terms misses the two middle products. Every term times every term.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Sign errors">
In $(3x - 4)(2x + 5)$, the $-4$ carries its sign into both of its products: $-8x$ and $-20$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- **Monomials:** multiply coefficients, add exponents of like bases.
- **Monomial × polynomial:** distribute to every term.
- **Polynomial × polynomial:** every term times every term, then collect like terms. Check by substituting a number.
