<Callout type="why">
Over $4$ days, a stock's price fell a total of $28$ riyals. What was the average change per day? That's $-28 \div 4$. Division is how you share a total — including a negative total — into equal parts.
</Callout>

## Division undoes multiplication

Every division has a matching multiplication:

$$
\frac{a}{b} = c \quad \text{exactly when} \quad c \cdot b = a
$$

For example, $\dfrac{-28}{4} = -7$ because $(-7) \cdot 4 = -28$. That gives us the sign rules for free — they are the same as for multiplication.

<Callout type="tip" title="The sign rules for division">
- **Same signs** → the quotient is **positive**: $\dfrac{-42}{-6} = 7$
- **Different signs** → the quotient is **negative**: $\dfrac{42}{-6} = -7$
</Callout>

## Dividing with zero

<Callout type="definition" title="Zero in a division">
- **Zero divided by a non-zero number is $0$:** $\dfrac{0}{b} = 0$, because $0 \cdot b = 0$.
- **Division by zero is undefined:** $\dfrac{a}{0}$ has no answer. If ${a \ne 0}$, no number times $0$ gives $a$. If $a = 0$, *every* number works, so there's no single answer either.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="0 on top is fine; 0 on the bottom is not">
$\dfrac{0}{5} = 0$, but $\dfrac{5}{0}$ is undefined. It's easy to mix them up — always ask: *what times the bottom gives the top?*
</Callout>

## Reciprocals

<Callout type="definition" title="Reciprocal">
Two numbers whose **product is $1$** are **reciprocals** (also called *multiplicative inverses*). The reciprocal of $\dfrac{a}{b}$ is $\dfrac{b}{a}$, as long as $a$ and $b$ are not $0$.

$$
\frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{2} = 1 \qquad -4 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = 1 \qquad 0.25 \cdot 4 = 1
$$
</Callout>

A number and its reciprocal always have the **same sign** (their product, $1$, is positive). And $0$ has **no** reciprocal, because $0$ times anything is $0$, never $1$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Reciprocal vs. opposite">
The **opposite** of $5$ is $-5$ (they add to $0$). The **reciprocal** of $5$ is $\tfrac{1}{5}$ (they multiply to $1$). Don't mix them up.
</Callout>

## Dividing by multiplying

Dividing by a number is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal:

$$
a \div b = a \cdot \frac{1}{b}
$$

This is the familiar rule for fractions: **keep, change, flip**.

## Signs in fractions

Because a negative divided by a positive and a positive divided by a negative both give a negative, these three fractions are equal:

$$
-\frac{a}{b} \;=\; \frac{-a}{b} \;=\; \frac{a}{-b}
$$

But $\dfrac{-a}{-b} = \dfrac{a}{b}$ — two negatives cancel.

## Watch out for these

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">
1. **Saying $\tfrac{5}{0} = 0$.** Division by zero is **undefined**.
2. **Changing the sign of a reciprocal.** The reciprocal of $-\tfrac{2}{3}$ is $-\tfrac{3}{2}$, not $\tfrac{3}{2}$.
3. **Flipping the wrong fraction.** In $\tfrac{a}{b} \div \tfrac{c}{d}$, keep the first fraction and flip the **second**.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Division has the same sign rules as multiplication: same signs → positive, different signs → negative.
- $\frac{0}{b} = 0$ for ${b \ne 0}$; $\frac{a}{0}$ is **undefined**.
- Reciprocals multiply to $1$. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.
- $-\frac{a}{b} = \frac{-a}{b} = \frac{a}{-b}$.
