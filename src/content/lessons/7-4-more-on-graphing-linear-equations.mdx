Railroad tracks never meet — they're parallel. The corner of a page is a right angle — perpendicular edges. You can tell both relationships between lines just by comparing their slopes.
</Callout>

## Horizontal and vertical lines

- $y = b$ is a **horizontal** line: every point has $y$-coordinate $b$. Its slope is $0$.
- $x = a$ is a **vertical** line: every point has $x$-coordinate $a$. Its slope is **undefined** (the run is $0$).

<Callout type="tip" title="Which is which?">
$y = 3$ — "$y$ is always $3$" — so the line is flat, at height $3$. $x = 3$ — "$x$ is always $3$" — so the line stands straight up, $3$ units right of the $y$-axis.
</Callout>

## Parallel and perpendicular lines

- **Parallel** lines have the **same slope** (and different $y$-intercepts). Vertical lines are parallel to each other.
- **Perpendicular** lines have slopes whose **product is $-1$** — each is the opposite reciprocal of the other. A horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="y = 3 or x = 3?">
$y = 3$ is **horizontal** (every $y$ is $3$). $x = 3$ is **vertical**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Half of the perpendicular rule">
The perpendicular slope to $\frac{2}{3}$ is $-\frac{3}{2}$: flip **and** change the sign.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Comparing slopes too early">
Solve each equation for $y$ before comparing. $2x + y = 1$ has slope $-2$, not $2$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $y = b$: horizontal, slope $0$. $x = a$: vertical, slope undefined.
- Parallel: equal slopes. Perpendicular: slopes multiply to $-1$.
