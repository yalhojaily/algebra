<Callout type="why">
The formula $d = rt$ tells you how far you'll travel. But on a road trip the question is often different: *the city is $420$ km away and I drive at $105$ km/h — how long will it take?* You need $t$ by itself: $t = \dfrac{d}{r}$. Rearranging formulas lets one fact answer many different questions.
</Callout>

## Evaluating formulas

A **formula** is an equation that uses letters to represent a relationship between quantities. To evaluate it, substitute the known values and follow the order of operations.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Simple interest"
  problem="Use $I = Prt$ to find the interest on $P = 5000$ riyals at a rate $r = 0.04$ per year for $t = 3$ years."
  steps={[
    { show: '$I = 5000 \\cdot 0.04 \\cdot 3$', why: 'Substitute.' },
    { show: '$= 200 \\cdot 3 = 600$', why: 'Multiply left to right.' },
  ]}
  answer="The interest is $600$ riyals."
/>

## Solving a formula for a letter

To solve a formula for a letter, use the **same steps** as for any equation — just treat the other letters as if they were numbers. Find what's being done to the letter you want, and undo it in reverse order.

<Callout type="tip" title="Compare with an equation you already know how to solve">
$$\begin{aligned} 2x + 3 &= 11 & \qquad P &= 2l + 2w \\ 2x &= 11 - 3 & P - 2w &= 2l \\ x &= \frac{11 - 3}{2} & \frac{P - 2w}{2} &= l \end{aligned}$$

Same moves on both sides: subtract, then divide.
</Callout>

Pick a formula and a letter, and step through it. The highlighted letter is the one you're solving for.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Solving for a letter"
  problem="Solve $A = \dfrac{a + b}{2}$ for $b$. (This formula gives the average of two numbers.)"
  steps={[
    { show: '$2A = a + b$', why: 'Multiply both sides by $2$ to clear the fraction.' },
    { show: '$2A - a = b$', why: 'Subtract $a$ from both sides.' },
  ]}
  answer="$b = 2A - a$"
/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Get the letter completely alone">
$bh = 2A$ is true, but it isn't solved for $h$ — $h$ is still multiplied by $b$. The letter you're solving for must be **alone** on one side, and **must not appear** on the other side.
</Callout>

## Rearrange first, then use

If you need to answer the same kind of question many times, rearrange the formula once and then just substitute.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Celsius from Fahrenheit"
  problem="It is $95^\circ$F. What is that in degrees Celsius? Use $F = \tfrac{9}{5}C + 32$."
  steps={[
    { show: '$C = \\tfrac{5}{9}(F - 32)$', why: 'Solve the formula for $C$ (see the Temperature formula above).' },
    { show: '$C = \\tfrac{5}{9}(95 - 32) = \\tfrac{5}{9} \\cdot 63$', why: 'Substitute $F = 95$.' },
    { show: '$= 35$', why: '' },
  ]}
  answer="$95^\circ$F is $35^\circ$C."
/>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Not getting the letter alone">
$2w = P - 2l$ is not finished: divide by $2$ to get $w = \frac{P - 2l}{2}$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Dividing only one term">
From $P = 2l + 2w$, dividing by $2$ gives $\frac{P}{2} = l + w$ — **both** terms on the right are divided.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Mixing units">
In $d = rt$ with $r$ in km/h, $t$ must be in **hours**: $30$ minutes is $0.5$ h.
</Callout>

## Summary

- A **formula** relates quantities with letters. Evaluate it by substituting.
- To **solve for a letter**, treat the other letters as numbers and use the addition and multiplication principles.
- The answer must have that letter alone on one side, and nowhere on the other.
