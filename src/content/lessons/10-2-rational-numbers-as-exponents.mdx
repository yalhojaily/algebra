<Callout type="why">
What should $9^{1/2}$ mean? If the product rule still works, $9^{1/2} \cdot 9^{1/2} = 9^{1} = 9$. So $9^{1/2}$ is a number whose square is $9$ — it must be $\sqrt{9} = 3$. Rational exponents are defined so that the old rules keep working.
</Callout>

## The definitions

$$a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a} \qquad a^{m/n} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m = \sqrt[n]{a^m} \qquad a^{-m/n} = \frac{1}{a^{m/n}}$$

The **denominator** is the root; the **numerator** is the power.

## Converting

To type a rational exponent, use parentheses: `x^(2/3)`. Without them, `x^2/3` means $\frac{x^2}{3}$.

## Laws of exponents

The laws from Chapter 4 hold for rational exponents (with positive bases):

$$a^m a^n = a^{m+n} \qquad \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \qquad (a^m)^n = a^{mn}$$

## Summary

- $a^{m/n} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^m$: denominator → root, numerator → power.
- $a^{-m/n}$ is the reciprocal of $a^{m/n}$.
- The usual exponent laws apply; fractions need common denominators.
