<Callout type="why">
Biologists estimate the energy an animal needs per day as $70W^{3/4}$ kilocalories, where $W$ is its mass. What does an exponent of $\frac{3}{4}$ even mean? This lesson answers that, and shows why fractional exponents are so useful: with them, roots obey the same laws of exponents you learned in Chapter 4.
</Callout>

## Why a^(1/n) is a root

What should $9^{1/2}$ mean? If the product rule $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$ is to keep working, then

$$9^{1/2} \cdot 9^{1/2} = 9^{1/2 + 1/2} = 9^1 = 9.$$

So $9^{1/2}$ is a number whose square is $9$: it must be $\sqrt{9} = 3$. In the same way, $8^{1/3} \cdot 8^{1/3} \cdot 8^{1/3} = 8$, so $8^{1/3} = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$. And the power rule $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ gives the rest:

$$a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a} \qquad a^{m/n} = \left(a^{1/n}\right)^m = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m = \sqrt[n]{a^m} \qquad a^{-m/n} = \frac{1}{a^{m/n}}$$

The **denominator** is the root; the **numerator** is the power.

## Converting

$$x^{3/5} = \left(\sqrt[5]{x}\right)^3 = \sqrt[5]{x^3}, \qquad \sqrt[4]{y^3} = y^{3/4}, \qquad \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} = x^{-1/2}$$

To type a rational exponent, use parentheses: `x^(2/3)`. Without them, `x^2/3` means $\frac{x^2}{3}$.

## Laws of exponents

The laws from Chapter 4 hold for rational exponents (with positive bases):

$$a^m a^n = a^{m+n} \qquad \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n} \qquad (a^m)^n = a^{mn} \qquad (ab)^n = a^n b^n$$

Adding and subtracting fractions is the only new skill — use a common denominator.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="a^(1/2) is not a/2">
$16^{1/2} = 4$, not $8$. A fractional exponent is a root, not a multiplication.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="A negative exponent doesn't make a negative number">
$27^{-2/3} = \frac{1}{9}$, a positive number. The minus sign in the exponent means "reciprocal".
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Root and power swapped">
$8^{2/3}$ is a cube root, then a square ($4$) — not a square root, then a cube ($\sqrt{8}^3 \approx 22.6$).
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Typing without parentheses">
`x^2/3` is $\frac{x^2}{3}$. Type `x^(2/3)`.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a}$ is forced by the product rule; $a^{m/n} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^m$: denominator → root, numerator → power.
- $a^{-m/n}$ is the reciprocal of $a^{m/n}$.
- The usual exponent laws apply; add and subtract fractional exponents with common denominators.
- Converting to exponents can simplify radicals: $\sqrt[6]{x^3} = \sqrt{x}$.
