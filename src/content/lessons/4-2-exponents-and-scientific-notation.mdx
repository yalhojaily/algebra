<Callout type="why">
The Sun is about $150{,}000{,}000{,}000$ m away. A virus is about $0.0000001$ m wide. Counting zeros in numbers like these is slow and error-prone. Scientific notation — $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ and $1 \times 10^{-7}$ — makes them readable, and the exponent rules make them easy to calculate with: how long does sunlight take to reach us?
</Callout>

## Raising powers to powers

Write out the factors again: $(x^3)^2 = x^3 \cdot x^3 = x^6$ — **two groups of three** factors. In the same way, $(ab)^3 = ab \cdot ab \cdot ab = a^3b^3$: every factor inside appears three times.

<ExponentLab client:visible />

<Callout type="tip" title="Three power rules">
$$(a^m)^n = a^{mn} \qquad (ab)^n = a^n b^n \qquad \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n}$$

To raise a product or quotient to a power, raise **every factor** to that power.
</Callout>

## Scientific notation

<Callout type="definition" title="Scientific notation">
A number is in scientific notation when it is written as

$$M \times 10^n, \qquad 1 \le M < 10, \;\; n \text{ an integer}$$
</Callout>

Multiplying by $10^n$ moves the decimal point $n$ places: right for positive $n$, left for negative $n$. So large numbers get positive exponents, and numbers between $0$ and $1$ get negative exponents.

## Calculating in scientific notation

Multiply (or divide) the decimal parts and use the product (or quotient) rule on the powers of 10. If the decimal part ends up outside $[1, 10)$, adjust.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Multiply, then adjust"
  problem="Multiply: $\;(6.3 \times 10^5)(4 \times 10^{-2})$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$(6.3 \\times 4) \\times 10^{5 + (-2)} = 25.2 \\times 10^3$', why: '' },
    { show: '$= 2.52 \\times 10^1 \\times 10^3 = 2.52 \\times 10^4$', why: '$25.2$ is too big — move the point left one place and add $1$ to the exponent.' },
  ]}
/>

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Divide, then adjust"
  problem="Divide: $\;\dfrac{1.2 \times 10^{-4}}{4 \times 10^{3}}$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$= \\dfrac{1.2}{4} \\times 10^{-4 - 3} = 0.3 \\times 10^{-7}$', why: 'Subtract the exponents.' },
    { show: '$= 3 \\times 10^{-8}$', why: '$0.3$ is too small — move the point right one place and subtract $1$ from the exponent.' },
  ]}
/>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Forgetting the number">
$(3x^2)^2 = 9x^4$, not $3x^4$. The coefficient is a factor too, so it gets squared.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="The power rule is for products, not sums">
$(xy)^2 = x^2y^2$, but $(x + y)^2 \ne x^2 + y^2$. (Lesson 4.6 shows what $(x + y)^2$ really is.)
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Not adjusting">
$25.2 \times 10^3$ is not in scientific notation: the first part must be at least $1$ and less than $10$. Rewrite it as $2.52 \times 10^4$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$, $\;(ab)^n = a^nb^n$, $\;(a/b)^n = a^n/b^n$ — because every factor is repeated.
- Scientific notation: $M \times 10^n$ with $1 \le M < 10$; large numbers have positive exponents, numbers between 0 and 1 negative ones.
- To multiply or divide, work on the decimal parts and the powers of 10 separately, then adjust.
