<Callout type="why">
A bakery has 16 hours of oven time and 14 hours of decorating time a week. Which combinations of cakes and pies can it make? Each limit is an inequality in two variables, and together they form a system whose solutions are a whole **region** of the plane. The corners of that region turn out to be the most interesting points — they are where businesses look for the best plan.
</Callout>

## Solutions are regions

A solution of $y > 2x - 1$ is an ordered pair that makes it true. $(0, 0)$ is one: $0 > -1$ ✓. $(3, 1)$ is not: $1 > 5$ ✗.

Why does a whole region work? The line $y = 2x - 1$ is where the two sides are **equal**. Above the line, $y$ is bigger than $2x - 1$; below it, smaller. As you move around the plane, the inequality can only switch from true to false by crossing the line. So the line splits the plane into two **half-planes**: one where every point is a solution and one where none is.

## Graphing an inequality

1. Graph the **boundary** line (replace the inequality sign with $=$). Use a **solid** line for $\le$ or $\ge$ (the line is included) and a **dashed** line for $<$ or $>$ (it is not).
2. Pick a **test point** not on the line — the origin is easiest if the line doesn't pass through it.
3. If the test point makes the inequality true, shade its side; otherwise shade the other side.

<Callout type="tip" title="Shortcut for y = mx + b form">
When the inequality is solved for $y$: $y >$ or $y \ge$ means shade **above** the line; $y <$ or $y \le$ means shade **below**. ($2x - 3y < 6$ becomes $y > \frac{2}{3}x - 2$ — above, as we found.)
</Callout>

## Systems of inequalities

A solution of a system makes **every** inequality true, so the solution set is where **all** the shaded regions overlap. The corners of that region are its **vertices**. A vertex lies on two boundary lines, so you find it by solving the system of the two boundary **equations** (with the methods of Chapter 8).

## In a real context

Limited resources — hours, money, materials — each give an inequality. The region is the set of **possible plans**; a plan works only if its point is inside the region.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Cakes and pies"
  problem="A bakery makes $x$ cakes and $y$ pies a week. Each cake needs $2$ hours of oven time and $1$ hour of decorating; each pie needs $1$ hour of oven time and $2$ hours of decorating. There are $16$ oven hours and $14$ decorating hours. (a) Can it make $5$ cakes and $4$ pies? (b) If it makes $6$ cakes, how many pies at most?"
  steps={[
    { show: '**Familiarize.** $x$ = cakes, $y$ = pies; both are whole numbers $\\ge 0$.', why: 'Each resource limit becomes one inequality.' },

    { show: '**Solve (a).** $2(5) + 4 = 14 \\le 16$ ✓ and $5 + 2(4) = 13 \\le 14$ ✓', why: 'Test the point $(5, 4)$ in every inequality.' },
    { show: '**Solve (b).** $x = 6$: oven $12 + y \\le 16 \\Rightarrow y \\le 4$; decorating $6 + 2y \\le 14 \\Rightarrow y \\le 4$. So $y \\le 4$.', why: 'Both limits must hold.' },

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Solid or dashed">
$\le$ and $\ge$: solid (points on the line count). $<$ and $>$: dashed.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Using the shortcut on the wrong form">
"$>$ means above" works only when the inequality is solved for $y$. $-y > x$ is $y < -x$: **below**. When in doubt, use a test point.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="A test point on the line">
If the boundary passes through $(0, 0)$, the origin tells you nothing. Pick another point, like $(1, 0)$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Forgetting x ≥ 0 and y ≥ 0">
You can't bake $-2$ cakes. In real problems the quantities are usually non-negative — two more inequalities.
</Callout>

## Summary

- The boundary line splits the plane; one side is all solutions, the other none. A test point decides which.
- Solid boundary for $\le, \ge$; dashed for $<, >$.
- A system's solutions are the overlap; vertices come from solving pairs of boundary **equations**.
- In applications, each limited resource is an inequality, and the region is the set of possible plans.
