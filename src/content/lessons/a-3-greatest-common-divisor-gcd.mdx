<Callout type="why">
Reducing $\frac{36}{60}$ to lowest terms in one step means dividing by the largest number that goes into both — the greatest common divisor. The GCD is also the "greatest common factor" you pull out when factoring polynomials in Chapter 5. (It is also called the GCF or HCF.)
</Callout>

## Factors and common factors

A **factor** (or **divisor**) of a number divides it with no remainder.

- Factors of 18: $\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{2}, \mathbf{3}, \mathbf{6}, 9, 18$
- Factors of 24: $\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{2}, \mathbf{3}, 4, \mathbf{6}, 8, 12, 24$

The **common factors** are $1, 2, 3, 6$, and the greatest is $\text{GCD}(18, 24) = 6$.

## Using prime factorizations

The GCD is built only from primes the numbers **share**, each to the **lowest** power it appears in both.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Prime-factorization method"
  problem="Find the GCD of 72 and 120."
  steps={[
    { show: '$72 = 2^3 \\cdot 3^2 \\qquad 120 = 2^3 \\cdot 3 \\cdot 5$', why: '' },
    { show: '$\\text{shared primes: } 2, 3$', why: '5 is only in 120, so it is not a common factor.' },
    { show: '$2^3 \\cdot 3^1 = 24$', why: 'Lowest power of each shared prime: $2^3$ in both, $3^1$ (120 has only one 3).' },
  ]}
/>

In the Venn diagram, the GCD is the product of the **middle** — the primes both numbers share. Compare with the LCM, which multiplies everything.

<PrimeVenn client:visible focus="gcd" />

| | GCD | LCM |
|---|---|---|
| Which primes | only the shared ones | all of them |
| Which power | lowest | highest |
| Size | at most the smaller number | at least the larger number |

And always: $\text{GCD}(a, b) \cdot \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \cdot b$.

## Reducing fractions with the GCD

Divide the numerator and denominator by their GCD and the fraction is in lowest terms in one step.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Reduce a fraction"
  problem="Write $\dfrac{36}{60}$ in lowest terms."
  steps={[
    { show: '$36 = 2^2 \\cdot 3^2 \\qquad 60 = 2^2 \\cdot 3 \\cdot 5$', why: '' },
    { show: '$\\text{GCD} = 2^2 \\cdot 3 = 12$', why: '' },
    { show: '$\\dfrac{36 \\div 12}{60 \\div 12} = \\dfrac{3}{5}$', why: 'Dividing top and bottom by the same number doesn\'t change the value.' },
  ]}
/>

If you divide by a smaller common factor (say 6) you get $\frac{6}{10}$ — still not done. Dividing by the GCD finishes the job at once.

## GCD in problems

"What is the **largest** group size / tile size / number of identical kits that uses everything exactly?" is a GCD question.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning">
**Mixing up GCD and LCM.** The GCD divides both numbers, so it can't be larger than the smaller number. If your "GCD" of 12 and 18 is 36, you found the LCM. ($\text{GCD} = 6$.)
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Using highest powers or unshared primes.** For $\text{GCD}(72, 120)$, don't include the 5 (only 120 has it) and don't use $3^2$ (120 has only one 3).
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Saying the GCD is 0 when nothing is shared.** If two numbers share no prime factor — like 14 and 25 — their GCD is **1**. They are called *relatively prime*.
</Callout>

## Summary

- A common factor divides every number; the GCD is the greatest one.
- Listing: list factors in pairs and pick the largest common one.
- Primes: take only the shared primes, each to the lowest power.
- Divide numerator and denominator by their GCD to reduce a fraction in one step.
- $\text{GCD}(a, b) \cdot \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \cdot b$.
