<Callout type="why">
In Chapter 4 you multiplied: $3x(x + 5) = 3x^2 + 15x$. **Factoring** goes the other way — from $3x^2 + 15x$ back to $3x(x + 5)$. It's the key to solving equations like $x^2 = 5x$, which you'll do in Lesson 5.8.
</Callout>

## What it means to factor

To **factor** a polynomial means to write it as a **product**. A **factorization** of $15x^3$ is $3x \cdot 5x^2$; another is $15 \cdot x^3$. For a polynomial with several terms, the first thing to look for is a factor that **every term** shares.

The **greatest common factor (GCF)** of some monomials is:

- the largest whole number that divides every coefficient, times
- each variable that appears in **every** term, raised to its **smallest** exponent.

## Factoring out the GCF

Divide each term by the GCF, and write the GCF in front of parentheses. This is the distributive law in reverse.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Factor out the GCF"
  problem="Factor: $\;12x^5 - 18x^3 + 6x^2$"
  steps={[
    { show: 'GCF $= 6x^2$', why: 'The GCF of $12, 18, 6$ is $6$; the lowest power of $x$ is $x^2$.' },
    { show: '$12x^5 \\div 6x^2 = 2x^3,\\quad -18x^3 \\div 6x^2 = -3x,\\quad 6x^2 \\div 6x^2 = 1$', why: 'Divide every term by the GCF.' },
    { show: '$= 6x^2(2x^3 - 3x + 1)$', why: 'Check by multiplying back out.' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't lose the 1">
When the GCF is a whole term, that term leaves a $1$ behind: $6x^2(2x^3 - 3x + 1)$, not $6x^2(2x^3 - 3x)$. Multiply back to check — you should get the same number of terms you started with.
</Callout>

## Factoring by grouping

Sometimes the common factor is a whole **binomial**: $x^2(x + 3) + 5(x + 3) = (x + 3)(x^2 + 5)$. With four terms, try grouping them in pairs.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Factor by grouping"
  problem="Factor: $\;x^3 + 4x^2 - 3x - 12$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$(x^3 + 4x^2) + (-3x - 12)$', why: 'Group the first two terms and the last two terms.' },
    { show: '$x^2(x + 4) - 3(x + 4)$', why: 'Factor the GCF from each group. Factoring out $-3$ (not $3$) makes the two binomials match.' },
    { show: '$= (x + 4)(x^2 - 3)$', why: 'Now $(x + 4)$ is a common factor — factor it out.' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout type="tip" title="Match the binomials">
If the two groups give different binomials, like $(x + 4)$ and $(-x - 4)$, factor a negative out of the second group. If they still don't match, grouping in that order doesn't work.
</Callout>

<Practice client:visible lesson="5.1" skill="grouping" compact />

## Summary

- Factoring writes a polynomial as a product.
- **GCF** = largest common number × each shared variable to its lowest power.
- Factor it out by dividing each term; check by multiplying back.
- Four terms → try **grouping** in pairs, then factor out the common binomial.
