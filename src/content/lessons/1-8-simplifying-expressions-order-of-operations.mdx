<Callout type="why">
What is $2 + 3 \cdot 4$? If you go left to right you get $20$; if you multiply first you get $14$. A calculator, a spreadsheet, and a math teacher all say $14$. Mathematics needs **one** agreed reading of every expression, or nobody could communicate. That agreement is the **order of operations**.
</Callout>

## The opposite of a sum

What does the minus sign in front of parentheses do? It means "the opposite of", and the opposite of a sum is the sum of the opposites:

$$
-(a + b) = -a + (-b) = -a - b
$$

This is really the distributive law with $-1$: $\;-(a + b) = -1 \cdot (a + b)$.

<Callout type="tip" title="A minus sign in front of parentheses">
Remove the parentheses and **change the sign of every term inside**.

$$-(4x - 7y + 2) = -4x + 7y - 2$$
</Callout>

## Exponential notation

An **exponent** is shorthand for repeated multiplication:

$$
\underbrace{a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a}_{4 \text{ factors}} = a^4
$$

In $a^4$, $a$ is the **base** and $4$ is the **exponent**. Read it as "$a$ to the fourth power". Also, $a^1 = a$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Where does the exponent apply?">
An exponent applies **only to the thing directly in front of it**.

$$(-3)^2 = (-3)(-3) = 9 \qquad\text{but}\qquad -3^2 = -(3 \cdot 3) = -9$$

In $-3^2$ the base is $3$, not $-3$. Also, $(2x)^2 = 4x^2$, but $2x^2$ means $2 \cdot x \cdot x$.
</Callout>

## The order of operations

<Callout type="definition" title="Rules for order of operations">
1. Do all calculations **inside grouping symbols** — parentheses $( )$, brackets $[ ]$, braces $\{ \}$, fraction bars, and absolute-value bars — working from the **innermost** outward.
2. Evaluate all **exponential expressions**.
3. Do all **multiplications and divisions**, in order from **left to right**.
4. Do all **additions and subtractions**, in order from **left to right**.
</Callout>

Multiplication does **not** come before division — they share step 3 and you go left to right. The same is true for addition and subtraction in step 4. So $36 \div 4 \cdot 3 = 9 \cdot 3 = 27$, not $36 \div 12 = 3$.

## Nested grouping symbols

When grouping symbols are inside other grouping symbols, start with the **innermost** pair and work outward. Brackets $[ ]$ and braces $\{ \}$ mean the same as parentheses; they just make the nesting easier to read.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Inside out"
  problem="Simplify: $\;16 \div 2\,\{5 - [9 - (2 + 3)]\}$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$= 16 \\div 2\\,\\{5 - [9 - 5]\\}$', why: 'Innermost parentheses: $2 + 3 = 5$.' },
    { show: '$= 16 \\div 2\\,\\{5 - 4\\}$', why: 'Then the brackets: $9 - 5 = 4$.' },
    { show: '$= 16 \\div 2 \\cdot 1$', why: 'Then the braces: $5 - 4 = 1$.' },
    { show: '$= 8 \\cdot 1 = 8$', why: 'Divide and multiply from left to right.' },
  ]}
/>

## Watch out for these

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">
1. **Going strictly left to right.** $2 + 3 \cdot 4 = 14$. Multiply before adding.
2. **Multiplying before dividing.** $24 \div 4 \cdot 2 = 12$, not $3$. They are done left to right.
3. **$-3^2$ vs $(-3)^2$.** The first is $-9$, the second is $9$.
4. **Changing only one sign** when removing parentheses after a minus sign.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $-(a + b - c) = -a - b + c$: a minus sign in front changes **every** sign inside.
- $a^n$ means $n$ factors of $a$. The exponent applies only to the base right in front of it.
- **Order of operations:** grouping (innermost first) → exponents → multiply & divide (left to right) → add & subtract (left to right).
