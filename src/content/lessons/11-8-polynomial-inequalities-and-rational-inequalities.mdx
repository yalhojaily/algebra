<Callout type="why">
When is a thrown ball higher than 48 feet? For which production levels does a business make a profit? Questions like "when is this expression positive?" are inequalities, and the answer is usually one or more **intervals**. Linear inequalities (9.1) could be solved like equations; these can't — but a sign chart handles them all.
</Callout>

## Why a sign chart works

A polynomial's graph is a smooth, unbroken curve, so its value can change from positive to negative only by passing through $0$. A rational expression can also change sign where its denominator is $0$ (where it is undefined). Call these $x$-values the **critical values**. Between two neighboring critical values the sign can't change — so **one test value tells you the sign of the whole interval**.

## The method

1. Get $0$ on one side and factor.
2. Find the **critical values**: zeros of the numerator, and zeros of the denominator (for rational expressions).
3. They split the number line into intervals. Pick a **test value** in each interval and find the sign.
4. Keep the intervals with the sign you want. Include numerator zeros for $\le$ or $\ge$; **never** include denominator zeros.

## Rational inequalities

Don't multiply both sides by a denominator that contains $x$: you would need to know whether it is positive or negative to keep or reverse the sign. Instead, get $0$ on one side as a **single fraction** and use a sign chart.

  problem="Solve: $\;\dfrac{x + 1}{x - 3} \ge 0$"

    { show: 'Critical values: $-1$ (numerator) and $3$ (denominator)', why: '' },

    { show: '$(-\\infty, -1] \\cup (3, \\infty)$', why: '$-1$ makes the fraction $0$, allowed by $\\ge$. $3$ makes it undefined — never included.' },

<StepReveal

  problem="Solve: $\;\dfrac{x}{x - 2} < 3$"

  ]}

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Multiplying by the denominator">
Multiplying both sides of $\frac{x}{x - 2} < 3$ by $x - 2$ needs to know its sign. Use a single fraction and a sign chart instead.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Including a denominator zero">
Zeros of the denominator are never in the solution, even with $\le$ or $\ge$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="x² < 9 means x < 3">
That loses the left side and includes $x = -5$. The solution is $-3 < x < 3$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Forgetting to reverse">
Dividing $-16t^2 + 64t - 48 > 0$ by $-16$ gives $t^2 - 4t + 3 < 0$, with the sign reversed (9.1).
</Callout>

## Summary

- Signs can change only at critical values (zeros of the expression and of a denominator), so one test value per interval is enough.
- Get $0$ on one side, factor (or combine into one fraction), find critical values, test, keep the right intervals.
- Numerator zeros: included for $\le, \ge$. Denominator zeros: never included.
- Write the answer as a union of intervals.
