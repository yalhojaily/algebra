## Addition first, then multiplication

In $3x + 4 = 13$, two things were done to $x$: multiply by $3$, then add $4$. To undo them, work in **reverse order** — like taking off your shoes before your socks.

## The variable on both sides

Use the addition principle to move all the variable terms to **one** side and all the numbers to the **other**. It's usually easiest to collect variable terms on the side where the coefficient stays positive.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="The two gyms"
  problem="Solve: $\;120m + 50 = 135m$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$50 = 15m$', why: 'Subtract $120m$ from both sides.' },
    { show: '$m = \\dfrac{50}{15} = \\dfrac{10}{3} \\approx 3.3$', why: 'Divide both sides by $15$.' },
  ]}
  answer="The costs are equal after about $3\tfrac{1}{3}$ months; from month $4$ on, the first gym is cheaper."
/>

Solve these yourself. Plan your moves: first collect the $x$-terms, then the numbers, then divide.

Remove parentheses with the distributive law first. Then solve as before.

## Clearing fractions and decimals

Fractions are where most arithmetic errors happen. Avoid them: multiply **every term on both sides** by the least common denominator (LCD). For decimals, multiply by $10$, $100$, …

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Clear the fractions"
  problem="Solve: $\;\dfrac{2}{3}x - \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$6 \\cdot \\dfrac{2}{3}x - 6 \\cdot \\dfrac{1}{6} = 6 \\cdot \\dfrac{1}{2}$', why: 'The LCD of $3$, $6$ and $2$ is $6$. Multiply **every** term.' },
    { show: '$4x - 1 = 3$', why: 'No fractions left.' },
    { show: '$4x = 4$, so $x = 1$', why: '' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Multiply every term">
When clearing fractions, a term without a fraction must be multiplied too. In $\frac{x}{2} + 3 = 5$, multiplying by $2$ gives $x + 6 = 10$, not $x + 3 = 10$.
</Callout>

## No solution, or every number?

Sometimes the variable **disappears**. Look at what's left:

- A **false** statement such as $5 = 2$: the equation has **no solution**.
- A **true** statement such as $5 = 5$: **every real number** is a solution (an *identity*).

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="When the variable vanishes"
  problem="Solve: $\;3(x + 2) = 3x + 6\;$ and $\;3(x + 2) = 3x + 5$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$3x + 6 = 3x + 6 \\;\\Rightarrow\\; 6 = 6$', why: 'Always true: **every real number** is a solution.' },
    { show: '$3x + 6 = 3x + 5 \\;\\Rightarrow\\; 6 = 5$', why: 'Never true: **no solution**.' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout type="tip" title="An equation-solving procedure">
1. **Clear** fractions or decimals (multiply by the LCD or a power of 10).
2. **Remove** parentheses with the distributive law.
3. **Collect** like terms on each side.
4. Use the **addition principle** to get variable terms on one side and numbers on the other.
5. Use the **multiplication principle** to get the variable alone.
6. **Check** in the original equation.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Undo operations in reverse order: usually addition principle first, multiplication principle last.
- Collect variable terms on one side and numbers on the other.
- Clear fractions with the LCD and decimals with a power of $10$ — multiply **every** term.
- If the variable disappears: a true statement means all real numbers; a false one means no solution.
