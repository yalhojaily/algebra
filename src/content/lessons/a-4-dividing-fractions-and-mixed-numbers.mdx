## What dividing means

$6 \div 2$ asks: how many 2s fit into 6? Dividing fractions asks the same question. $\frac{3}{2} \div \frac{1}{4}$ asks: **how many quarters fit into one and a half?** The answer is 6.

## The reciprocal

Two numbers are **reciprocals** if their product is 1. Flip a fraction to get its reciprocal:

$$\frac{3}{8} \to \frac{8}{3} \qquad 5 = \frac{5}{1} \to \frac{1}{5} \qquad \frac{1}{4} \to 4$$

Zero has no reciprocal, because nothing times 0 is 1. That's why you can't divide by zero.

## Dividing fractions: keep, change, flip

$$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c}$$

**Keep** the first fraction, **change** $\div$ to $\times$, **flip** the second fraction. Then multiply and simplify as in A.1.

Why does flipping work? Multiplying top and bottom of $\dfrac{a/b}{c/d}$ by $\dfrac{d}{c}$ turns the bottom into 1. The full argument is in 6.2.

## Mixed numbers

A **mixed number** like $2\frac{3}{4}$ means $2 + \frac{3}{4}$. An **improper fraction** like $\frac{11}{4}$ has a numerator at least as large as its denominator. They are two names for the same number.

<MixedNumbers client:visible />

- **Mixed → improper:** multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and keep the denominator: $2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \cdot 4 + 3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$.
- **Improper → mixed:** divide. The quotient is the whole part and the remainder goes over the denominator: $\frac{11}{4}$ gives $11 \div 4 = 2$ remainder $3$, so $2\frac{3}{4}$.

To type a mixed number in the practice boxes, put a space between the whole number and the fraction: `2 3/4`.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning">
**Flipping the wrong fraction.** Only the divisor, the second fraction, gets flipped. $\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{5} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{5}{4}$, not $\frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{5}$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Dividing straight across.** $\frac{8}{9} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{3}$, and "top ÷ top, bottom ÷ bottom" happens to give the same thing here. It fails whenever the numbers don't divide evenly. Use the reciprocal every time.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Multiplying mixed numbers piece by piece.** $2\frac{1}{2} \times 1\frac{1}{3} \neq 2 \cdot 1 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3}$. That skips the cross terms. Convert to improper fractions first.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Converting with the wrong operation.** For $3\frac{2}{5}$ the numerator is $3 \cdot 5 + 2 = 17$, not $3 \cdot 2 + 5$. Each whole is 5 fifths.
</Callout>

## Summary

- The reciprocal of $\frac{c}{d}$ is $\frac{d}{c}$. A number times its reciprocal is 1.
- Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor: keep, change, flip.
- Mixed → improper: (whole × denominator + numerator) over the denominator.
- Improper → mixed: divide; the remainder goes over the denominator.
- Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing.
