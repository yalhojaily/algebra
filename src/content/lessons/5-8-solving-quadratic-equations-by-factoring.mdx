## The principle of zero products

> If $ab = 0$, then $a = 0$ or $b = 0$ (or both).

This works **only** for $0$. If $ab = 6$, $a$ and $b$ could be anything that multiplies to $6$.

## Solving by factoring

A **quadratic equation** has the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with $a \ne 0$. To solve one:

1. Get $0$ on one side (standard form).
2. Factor the other side.
3. Set each factor equal to $0$ and solve.
4. Check in the original equation.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Get 0 on one side first"
  problem="Solve: $\;x^2 = 5x + 14$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$x^2 - 5x - 14 = 0$', why: 'Subtract $5x$ and $14$ from both sides.' },
    { show: '$(x - 7)(x + 2) = 0$', why: 'Product $-14$, sum $-5$: the numbers are $-7$ and $2$.' },
    { show: '$x = 7 \\quad$ or $\\quad x = -2$', why: 'Check: $49 = 35 + 14$ ✓ and $4 = -10 + 14$ ✓' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't divide by x">
In $x^2 = 3x$, dividing both sides by $x$ gives just $x = 3$ and **loses** the solution $x = 0$. Instead: $x^2 - 3x = 0$, so $x(x - 3) = 0$, so $x = 0$ or $x = 3$.
</Callout>

## Solutions and x-intercepts

The solutions of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the $x$-values where the graph of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ crosses the $x$-axis — because those are the points where $y = 0$.

## Summary

- $ab = 0 \Rightarrow a = 0$ or $b = 0$.
- Standard form → factor → set each factor to $0$ → check.
- Never divide by a variable expression; you may lose solutions.
- Solutions = $x$-intercepts of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$.
