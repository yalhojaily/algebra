<Callout type="why">
Throw a ball up at $15$ m/s and its height after $t$ seconds is about $-5t^2 + 15t + 2$ meters. A company's profit, the area of a garden, the volume of a box — all are modeled by **polynomials**. They are the main objects of study for the next two chapters.
</Callout>

## What is a polynomial?

A **monomial** is a number, a variable, or a product of numbers and variables with **whole-number exponents**: $7$, $x$, $-3x^2$, $\tfrac{1}{2}a^3b$. A **polynomial** is a monomial or a sum of monomials:

$$4x^3 - 2x^2 + x - 9 \qquad\qquad -5t^2 + 15t + 2$$

Expressions like $\frac{1}{x}$ or $x^{-2}$ are **not** polynomials (the exponent must be $0, 1, 2, \ldots$).

<Callout type="definition" title="Polynomial vocabulary">
- **Terms** are the monomials being added: $4x^3$, $-2x^2$, $x$, $-9$.
- The **coefficient** of a term is its number part: $4$, $-2$, $1$, $-9$.
- The **degree of a term** is its exponent (a constant has degree $0$).
- The **degree of the polynomial** is the largest degree of its terms.
- The **leading term** has the highest degree; its coefficient is the **leading coefficient**.
- One term: **monomial**. Two: **binomial**. Three: **trinomial**.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="−x² when x is negative">
$-x^2$ means $-(x^2)$. With $x = -2$: $-(-2)^2 = -4$, **not** $+4$.
</Callout>

## Collecting like terms

Like terms have the same variable raised to the **same power**. Collect them by adding coefficients, then write the result in **descending order** (highest degree first).

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Collect and order"
  problem="Simplify: $\;3x^2 - 5 + 2x^3 - x^2 + 7x + 8$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$2x^3 + (3x^2 - x^2) + 7x + (-5 + 8)$', why: 'Group like terms.' },
    { show: '$= 2x^3 + 2x^2 + 7x + 3$', why: 'Descending order.' },
  ]}
/>

## Summary

- A polynomial is a sum of terms with whole-number exponents.
- Degree of a polynomial = highest degree of its terms. Leading term = term of highest degree.
- Evaluate by substituting (in parentheses!) and using the order of operations.
