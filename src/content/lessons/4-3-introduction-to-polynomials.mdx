## What is a polynomial?

A **monomial** is a number, a variable, or a product of numbers and variables with **whole-number exponents**: $7$, $x$, $-3x^2$, $\tfrac{1}{2}a^3b$. A **polynomial** is a monomial or a sum of monomials:

$$4x^3 - 2x^2 + x - 9 \qquad\qquad -5t^2 + 15t + 2$$

Expressions like $\frac{1}{x}$ or $x^{-2}$ are **not** polynomials: the exponent must be $0, 1, 2, \ldots$ (a variable in a denominator is a negative exponent in disguise).

<Callout type="definition" title="Polynomial vocabulary">
- **Terms** are the monomials being added: $4x^3$, $-2x^2$, $x$, $-9$. (Subtraction counts as adding a negative.)
- The **coefficient** of a term is its number part: $4$, $-2$, $1$, $-9$.
- The **degree of a term** is its exponent (a constant has degree $0$).
- The **degree of the polynomial** is the largest degree of its terms.
- The **leading term** has the highest degree; its coefficient is the **leading coefficient**.
- One term: **monomial**. Two: **binomial**. Three: **trinomial**.
</Callout>

## Evaluating polynomials

Substitute the value — in parentheses — and follow the order of operations.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Substitute carefully"
  problem="Evaluate $\;-x^2 + 3x - 4\;$ when $x = -2$."
  steps={[
    { show: '$-(-2)^2 + 3(-2) - 4$', why: 'Use parentheses around the negative number.' },
    { show: '$= -4 - 6 - 4 = -14$', why: '$(-2)^2 = 4$, so $-(-2)^2 = -4$.' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout type="warning" title="−x² when x is negative">
$-x^2$ means $-(x^2)$. With $x = -2$: $-(-2)^2 = -4$, **not** $+4$.
</Callout>

## Collecting like terms

**Like terms** have the same variable raised to the **same power**. Collect them by adding coefficients (the distributive law: $3x^2 - x^2 = (3 - 1)x^2$), then write the result in **descending order** — highest degree first.

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Collect and order"
  problem="Simplify: $\;3x^2 - 5 + 2x^3 - x^2 + 7x + 8$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$2x^3 + (3x^2 - x^2) + 7x + (-5 + 8)$', why: 'Group like terms.' },
    { show: '$= 2x^3 + 2x^2 + 7x + 3$', why: 'Descending order.' },
  ]}
/>

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Some terms cancel"
  problem="Simplify: $\;4a - 2a^3 + 7 - a + a^3$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$(-2a^3 + a^3) + (4a - a) + 7$', why: '' },
    { show: '$= -a^3 + 3a + 7$', why: 'There is no $a^2$ term — that\'s fine.' },
  ]}
/>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Combining unlike terms">
$x^2 + x$ is already simplified: it is not $x^3$ or $2x^2$. Only terms with the same power combine.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Degree = number of terms">
$x^5 + 1$ has two terms but degree $5$. The degree is the largest exponent.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="The first term isn't always the leading term">
In $4x^3 - 7x^5 + 2$, the leading term is $-7x^5$. Write the polynomial in descending order to see it.
</Callout>

## Summary

- A polynomial is a sum of terms with whole-number exponents.
- Degree of a polynomial = highest degree of its terms; leading term = term of highest degree.
- Evaluate by substituting (in parentheses!) and using the order of operations.
- Like terms have the same variable and power; collect them and write in descending order.
