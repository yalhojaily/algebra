<Callout type="why">
An equation like $x + 3 = 7$ has one answer. But many real questions don't: "How many hours can I work and still have time to study?" "What speeds are legal?" The answer is a whole **range** of numbers — and ranges are described by **inequalities**.
</Callout>

## Solutions and graphs

An **inequality** is a number sentence with $<$, $>$, $\le$, or $\ge$. A **solution** is any number that makes it true. Most inequalities have **infinitely many** solutions, so instead of listing them we **graph** them.

<Callout type="tip" title="Reading a graph">
- **Closed dot** ● at the boundary: the boundary **is** included ($\le$ or $\ge$).
- **Open dot** ○ at the boundary: the boundary is **not** included ($<$ or $>$).
- **Shade right** for greater than; **shade left** for less than.
</Callout>

We also write solution sets in **set-builder notation**: $\{x \mid x \ge -2\}$, read "the set of all $x$ such that $x$ is greater than or equal to $-2$."

## The addition principle for inequalities

Adding or subtracting the same number on both sides moves both numbers the same distance along the number line, so their **order doesn't change**.

<Callout type="definition" title="The addition principle for inequalities">
For any real numbers $a$, $b$, $c$: $\;a < b\;$ is equivalent to $\;a + c < b + c$. (The same holds for $>$, $\le$, $\ge$.)
</Callout>

## The multiplication principle — and the twist

Is $2 < 5$? Yes. Multiply both sides by $-1$: is $-2 < -5$? **No!** $-2$ is to the right of $-5$, so $-2 > -5$. Multiplying by a negative number **reverses the order**. See it happen:

<FlipDemo client:visible />

<Callout type="definition" title="The multiplication principle for inequalities">
For any real numbers $a$ and $b$:
- If $c$ is **positive**, $\;a < b\;$ is equivalent to $\;ac < bc$. The sign stays the same.
- If $c$ is **negative**, $\;a < b\;$ is equivalent to $\;ac > bc$. **The sign is reversed.**

(Dividing works the same way, because dividing by $c$ is multiplying by $\frac{1}{c}$.)
</Callout>

<StepReveal
  client:visible
  title="Dividing by a negative"
  problem="Solve: $\;-4y < 20$"
  steps={[
    { show: '$\\dfrac{-4y}{-4} > \\dfrac{20}{-4}$', why: 'Divide by $-4$ and **reverse** the sign.' },

/>

<Callout type="warning" title="The most common inequality mistake">
Forgetting to reverse the sign. Only **multiplying or dividing by a negative** reverses it — subtracting a number, or having a negative answer, does not.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Check with a test value">
Pick any number in your solution set (one that's easy, like $0$) and substitute it into the **original** inequality. If it doesn't make a true statement, look for a sign that should have been reversed.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Graph: closed dot for $\le, \ge$; open dot for $<, >$; shade toward the solutions.
- Set-builder notation: $\{x \mid x < 4\}$.
- Add or subtract on both sides freely — the sign stays.
- Multiply or divide by a **positive**: sign stays. By a **negative**: **reverse** the sign.
