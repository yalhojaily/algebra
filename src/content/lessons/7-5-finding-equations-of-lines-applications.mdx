## Point–slope form

The line with slope $m$ through the point $(x_1, y_1)$ is

$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

This comes straight from the slope formula: $\dfrac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = m$.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Signs in point–slope form">
Through $(-1, 4)$: $y - 4 = m(x - (-1)) = m(x + 1)$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Slope upside down">
$m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ — change in $y$ on top.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Predicting too far">
A linear model fits the data it came from. Predictions far outside that range are guesses — real growth often isn't linear forever.
</Callout>


## Summary

- Point–slope: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$; solve for $y$ to get $y = mx + b$.
- Two points: slope first, then point–slope.
- Parallel: same slope. Perpendicular: opposite reciprocal.
- Models: slope = rate of change; use the line to predict.
