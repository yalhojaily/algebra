## Adding polynomials

To add polynomials, remove the parentheses and **collect like terms**. Writing them in columns, one column per power of $x$, keeps like terms lined up.

## Opposites and subtraction

The **opposite** of a polynomial is what you add to it to get $0$. Since $-(a + b + c) = -a - b - c$ (the distributive law with $-1$), the opposite changes the sign of **every** term:

$$-(x^2 - 5x + 3) = -x^2 + 5x - 3$$

To **subtract** a polynomial, add its opposite — exactly as $7 - 3 = 7 + (-3)$.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Change every sign">
The most common mistake is changing only the first sign. In $-(-2x^5 + 5x^3 + 6)$, **all three** signs change.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Subtracting in the wrong order">
"Subtract $A$ from $B$" is $B - A$. "The difference of $A$ and $B$" is $A - B$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Adding exponents when adding terms">
$3x^2 + 2x^2 = 5x^2$, not $5x^4$. Adding like terms adds the **coefficients**; the power stays the same.
</Callout>

## Summary

- **Add:** collect like terms.
- **Opposite:** change the sign of every term (it is $-1$ times the polynomial).
- **Subtract:** add the opposite of the second polynomial, then collect like terms.
